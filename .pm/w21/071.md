# 071 — The day-to-day `bex-operator` role grants cluster-wide `pods/exec`, and six platform namespaces have no NetworkPolicy

Why: `pods/exec` on every pod is cluster-admin-equivalent through the service-account tokens mounted in pods, the operator's role is the one people use every day, and `scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh` does not test for it; six platform namespaces accept traffic from any pod in the cluster.

- **Source:** w21/m394 round 25 audit, hardening (Admission and RBAC), 2026-11-14.
- **Size:** about 1h30; the namespace list is the first step.

## Steps

1. Find the six: list every namespace and the NetworkPolicies in it; write the list into this note.
2. Decide per namespace the ingress it needs and add a default-deny plus the allow rules; check with `scripts/mock-cluster.sh`.
3. Remove `pods/exec` from the day-to-day role (or move it to a separate break-glass role), and make `scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh` fail when the kubeconfig's role can exec.

## Files

- lego/operator/config/rbac/role.yaml
- scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh
- deploy/gitops/base/ (the NetworkPolicy files of the six namespaces)

## Acceptance

- The role cannot create `pods/exec`, a test over the rendered RBAC says so, and every platform namespace has a default-deny ingress policy.
