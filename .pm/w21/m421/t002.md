## Objective

With the limit raised, the operator's real CPU demand is visible. Take a 30 s CPU profile and a heap profile on production through the loopback pprof endpoint and remove what the App controller spends on work that changes nothing, so a 27-App fleet does not need half a core and the queue wait scales with the fleet.

## Context

- 0.55 reconciles a second at 27 Apps is one pass per App every 47 s, 24 API reads a pass, 3,400 of 3,457 results `requeue_after`.
- Candidates, unmeasured: the background private-port sweeps (512 goroutines dialing up to 65,535 ports of about 20 services every 2 min), `heldBuilds` listing every App on each dispatch, the repeated `PodSecurity` warning handler (1,366 log lines in 2 h), per-pass re-applies of unchanged children, the requeue intervals themselves.
- Run the profile from the Mac: `kubectl port-forward pod/<manager> 6060` with the production kubeconfig fetched outside the repo; delete it and stop the port-forward afterwards.

## Steps

1. Profile (CPU 30 s, heap, goroutines) in a busy and a quiet minute; keep the numbers, not the profile files, in § Findings.
2. Fix the top consumers at the cause, each with a test that pins the behavior (envtest for the controller).
3. `make test`, `make lint`; ship; deploy; re-measure the operator's CPU, queue wait and reconcile median over 6 h.

## Files

- `lego/operator/internal/controller/`, `lego/operator/internal/portscan/`

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The profile's top five consumers are recorded with their share.
- [ ] After the deploy, the median App reconcile is 0.5 s or less and the queue p95 wait is 5 s or less over a 6 h window, with the operator's CPU use recorded.
- [ ] No change to what a service's status says, or when.

## Out of scope

- A larger `BEX_APP_RECONCILE_WORKERS` (ADR060 D1 retired the coupling to build capacity).
