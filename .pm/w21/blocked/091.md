# 091 — Narrow the 5 s token introspection window once production shows the Hydra and admission headroom

Why: w21/085 chose 5 s from a 3.7-day Hydra average of about 0.01 introspections a second and the per-credential admission budget of 60 a minute; a measured week at 5 s may justify 2 s, which makes a direct Hydra revocation take effect in under half the time.

- **Source:** w21/085, 2026-11-15. The floor is about 1 s: one miss a second per credential per replica is the whole admission budget (`lego/backend/internal/api/authadmission.go`, `BEX_AUTH_FAILURE_LIMIT`), so 2 s leaves a twofold margin and 5 s a fivefold one.

## Steps

1. **DONE 2026-11-16** (below). Add a counter for introspection cache misses and for admission sheds that land on a valid token (`bex_api` metrics, labelled by outcome) and a panel on the bex-api dashboard.
2. **WAITING until 2026-11-23 13:20 UTC** (below). After seven days on 5 s, read the peak misses per second per replica, the p99 Hydra introspection latency and the number of 429s that hit an authenticated caller.
3. **WAITING** (below). If the peak is under a tenth of the admission budget and Hydra's p99 stays under 50 ms, set `introspectionTTL` to 2 s, update `documentedRevocationLatency` and the number in `docs/ADR012-auth.md` §8d, and repeat the revoke-at-Hydra check on production. Otherwise record the measurement in §8d and close.

## Done when

- [ ] §8d states a number backed by a week of production measurement, and the revocation-latency test and the live check agree with it.

| Read | Query |
| --- | --- |
| **Q1x: peak misses per second per replica, 1-minute rate, pods older than 10 minutes (the rule's input)** | `max_over_time((sum by (pod) (rate(bex_api_auth_introspection_misses_total[1m])) and on (pod) ((time() - kube_pod_start_time{namespace="bex-system"}) > 600))[7d:1m])`: one value per pod, the rule reads the largest |

| Q1b: the largest 30-second burst per replica | `max_over_time(sum by (pod) (increase(bex_api_auth_introspection_misses_total[30s]))[7d:15s])` |
| Q4: misses by outcome over the week | `sum by (outcome) (increase(bex_api_auth_introspection_misses_total[7d]))` |
| **Q2: p99 of the Hydra introspection round trip, whole week (the rule's input)** | `histogram_quantile(0.99, sum by (le) (increase(bex_api_auth_hydra_introspection_duration_seconds_bucket{outcome="ok"}[7d])))` |
| Q2b: the share at or under 50 ms (exact, 0.05 is a bucket edge) | `sum(increase(bex_api_auth_hydra_introspection_duration_seconds_bucket{outcome="ok",le="0.05"}[7d])) / sum(increase(bex_api_auth_hydra_introspection_duration_seconds_count{outcome="ok"}[7d]))` |
| Q2c: Hydra calls and errors | `sum by (outcome) (increase(bex_api_auth_hydra_introspection_duration_seconds_count[7d]))` |
| **Q3b: 429s that hit an authenticated caller because of the window (a consistency check)** | `sum(increase(bex_api_auth_admission_sheds_total{credential="bearer",caller="authenticated"}[7d]))` |
| Q3: every shed, by credential, cause and caller | `sum by (credential, cause, caller) (increase(bex_api_auth_admission_sheds_total[7d]) > 0)` |

Use `increase`, not `rate`, over `[7d]`: pods are replaced by deploys, `rate` of a series that lived two days divides by its own span, and the sum of those rates would weight pods by lifetime. A new pod's series starts at 0 (registered at zero), so its first events are counted. `credential="session"` sheds are context only: a Kratos session is never cached, so its sheds are not caused by the introspection window. The dashboard's p99 panel reads one hour and is noisy at this traffic: use Q2.

**The decision rule (from the note above, unchanged).** The admission budget is `BEX_AUTH_FAILURE_LIMIT`, 60 a minute per credential per replica, so a tenth of it is **0.1 a second, 6 a minute**. A replica's total over all its credentials is an upper bound on any one credential's rate, which is why the rule can read it: no per-credential label exists, by design.

- If **Q1x's largest value is under 0.1 a second** and **Q2 is under 0.05 seconds**, take the narrowing branch.
- Otherwise take the other branch.
- Q3b is not a condition of the rule. It is a check on it: a shed on an authenticated bearer by `credential` means one credential spent its whole budget, which a peak under a tenth cannot be. A nonzero Q3b with Q1x under 0.1 means the replica total hid a hot credential: take the other branch and say so in §8d. Q1 and Q1b are recorded beside Q1x so the cold starts and bursts are on the record, not hidden.

**Does w21/090 change the urgency? It lowers it, and leaves the rule alone.** The operator command (`/api revoke-oauth-grant`, [runbook](../../../docs/runbooks/oauth-grant-cutoff.md)) gives an incident a cut-off with no window at all for a person's grant, measured on production on 2026-11-16 (a warm token refused at the next request, where a Hydra-only revocation was still accepted at 4.2 s), and an API key's revocation through bex already had none. So no incident has to wait for this number, and nothing is lost by waiting a week: what 5 s still costs is a revocation made at Hydra directly by something that is neither bex nor the command (an admin call, or the client's own RFC 7009 call), which is refused within 5 s instead of 2 s. The decision rule and its thresholds are what they were: the command changes what the window is _for_, not what 2 s costs in Hydra calls and admission budget.
