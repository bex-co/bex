# 071 — The day-to-day `bex-operator` role grants cluster-wide `pods/exec`, and six platform namespaces have no NetworkPolicy

Why: `pods/exec` on every pod is cluster-admin-equivalent through the service-account tokens mounted in pods, the operator's role is the one people use every day, and `scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh` does not test for it; six platform namespaces accept traffic from any pod in the cluster.

- **Source:** w21/m394 round 25 audit, hardening (Admission and RBAC), 2026-11-14.
- **Size:** about 1h30; the namespace list is the first step.

## Steps

1. Find the six: list every namespace and the NetworkPolicies in it; write the list into this note.
2. Decide per namespace the ingress it needs and add a default-deny plus the allow rules; check with `scripts/mock-cluster.sh`.
3. Remove `pods/exec` from the day-to-day role (or move it to a separate break-glass role), and make `scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh` fail when the kubeconfig's role can exec.

## Files

- lego/operator/config/rbac/role.yaml
- scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh
- deploy/gitops/base/ (the NetworkPolicy files of the six namespaces)

## Acceptance

- The role cannot create `pods/exec`, a test over the rendered RBAC says so, and every platform namespace has a default-deny ingress policy.

## Blocked (2026-11-14)

Two gates, one of them a decision only the user can make and one a cluster this session could not reach. Nothing in the note was changed; what was learned is recorded so the next pass starts from it.

- **Gate 1, a decision: whether the day-to-day credential keeps `pods/exec`.** It is deliberate, not an oversight. `deploy/gitops/base/operator-daytoday-rbac.yaml` grants "interactive debugging: logs, exec, port-forward, attach" cluster-wide, `scripts/operator-kubeconfig.sh` says its purpose is "read + exec + job-cleanup access without cluster-admin", and ADR019 makes it the alternative to `system:masters` for everyday work. The existing mitigation is a 12 h token (codex-security round-6 #5). Removing exec sends every debugging session to the break-glass admin kubeconfig, which defeats the credential's purpose; keeping it leaves the finding, which needs a stolen token. Options: (a) remove it and make exec a break-glass act; (b) a separate `bex-operator-exec` role and kubeconfig, minted on request with a shorter TTL (`operator-kubeconfig.sh --exec`), so the default credential cannot exec and debugging stays possible; (c) keep it and record the residual in ADR019 and ADR110, with Kubernetes audit logging of `pods/exec` as the compensating control. Recommendation: (b). The rendered-RBAC test the acceptance asks for follows whichever is chosen.
- **Gate 2, a cluster: the NetworkPolicies cannot be verified without one.** The note's step 1 asked for the six namespaces from a live list. From the manifests under `deploy/`, the namespaces with no NetworkPolicy object are `argocd`, `bex-capi`, `cert-manager`, `cnpg-system`, `dashboard`, `kpack` and `traefik` (`auth`, `bex-build`, `bex-registry`, `bex-system`, `monitoring`, `opensandbox-system` and `secrets` have one); the audit's six were counted on the live cluster, so confirm there which of these is not one of them. A default-deny ingress policy is not safe to write blind for most of them: `cert-manager`, `cnpg-system`, `kpack` and `bex-capi` host admission or conversion webhooks the API server calls, and whether a NetworkPolicy can select that traffic depends on the CNI and on where the control plane runs; `traefik` is the public edge and needs ports 80 and 443 from everywhere, health checks from the load balancer, and its own metrics scrape; `argocd` and `dashboard` are reached through Traefik. A wrong policy here is an outage that Argo applies by itself. It needs `scripts/mock-cluster.sh`, which could not be brought up where this ran (no `kind` or `clusterctl`, helm artifact downloads answering 504; see w21/079), or read access to the production cluster.
- **Next action:** when the user has chosen (a), (b) or (c) for exec, and a cluster is available, `/pm promote w21/071` as two tasks: the RBAC change with its rendered-RBAC test and the `operator-kubeconfig.sh` check, and one NetworkPolicy per namespace, each applied on the cluster with its webhook and ingress path exercised first.
