# 076 — bex refuses PORT 18014 where Render documents only 18012, 18013 and 19099 as reserved

Why: Render's web services page lists the reserved ports, and bex's list is one longer because its own edge-cache sidecar listens on 18014. A service that binds 18014 on Render works there and is refused here with `ENVIRONMENT_VARIABLE_PORT_INVALID`, a customer-visible difference that ADR004 and ADR089 record but ADR018, the register of divergences with the user's sign-off, does not.

- **Source:** w21/m396 t006 Render parity check, 2026-11-14. Render's wording read on 2026-11-14 at `https://render.com/docs/web-services`: "The following ports are reserved by Render and cannot be used:" followed by `18012`, `18013` and `19099`; "The default value of `PORT` is `10000` for all Render web services."
- **Evidence:** `lego/types/v1alpha1/image_network.go:61` (`IsReservedImagePort`, the four ports) feeds `core.ValidPortValue` (`core/config.go:202`); the corpus `env-value.json` pins 18012, 18013, 18014 and 19099 as refused and 18015 as accepted. ADR004:402 and ADR089-render-container-compatibility.md name 18014 (ADR004:402: "A `PORT` of 18014 is ignored, an explicit port of 18014 is refused"); ADR018:184 records PORT validity and "platform-reserved port" without listing the ports.
- **Size:** about 30m for the record, longer if the sidecar moves.

## Steps

1. Check what listens on 18014 and how a service binding it would fail (a port clash in the pod, or a silent redirect).
2. The parity-sufficient choice is to move the sidecar to a port Render also reserves or to one outside the range a service may choose, so the refused set equals Render's three ports. If that is not possible now, record the extra port in ADR018 as a divergence and ask for the user's sign-off, since a divergence stands only with it.
3. Keep `isValidPortValue` / `RESERVED_PLATFORM_PORTS` in `dashboard/src/features/services/lib/environment-draft.ts`, `core.ValidPortValue` and `env-value.json` in step.
