## Result (2026-11-15): Contract B walked on production, steps 1 to 5 done

Production ran the images pinned to digest `sha256:7e70d397bbb2d6b98cd6aed0f76515f5c54055361923e5e4c0621fce6171693f` (the Secrets installed, bex-api and bex-objectgw Ready, no restart during the walk), the tenant was `bex-canary`, and the client was stock **wrangler 4.162.0** with the three variables. Everything below, with the sha256 values and byte counts, is recorded in ADR112 § Not verified ("bex, settled 2026-11-15 on production (w21/m416 t013, Contract B)"); this is the summary.

- **Step 1.** `bucket create` (default and `--location weur`), `list`, `info`, `delete`; `object put`, `get`, `delete` with `--remote` at 0 B, 1 B, 5 MiB and 300 MiB with equal sha256; `cors list|set|delete`; `lifecycle list|add|set|remove`. All succeeded, and the error paths answer as designed (`10008` non-empty, `10006`, `10007`, `10005`, `10073`).
- **A real bug, fixed and shipped (6316803e1).** The first 300 MiB put through `api.bex.co` was refused in 0.5 s by **Cloudflare's own proxy** (HTML `413`): the edge refuses any request body over 100 MiB (104,857,600 bytes pass, 104,857,601 do not). wrangler's 300 MiB objects could never reach bex through that host. The same routes are now served on the DNS-only `objects.onbex.co` (one IngressRoute in `lego/operator/config/api`, priority 10000, `/cloudflare/client/v4/` only; the label is reserved as a service slug; `scripts/gitops-validate.sh` pins it). Regression tests: `TestReservedIncludesTheDirectAPIHost`, the real-Postgres `TestPGAServiceCannotTakeAPublicBucketsHost` (both fail with the reservation reverted), and the gitops-validate check (six mutations of the rendered route each fail it). Argo adopted the route (Synced, Healthy, tracked).
- **Step 2.** Through `objects.onbex.co` (and, before it existed, straight to Traefik with `api.bex.co` resolved to the load balancer) a 300 MiB put and get completed in 27 to 36 s with the sha256 equal, bex-api memory staying at 67 to 74 MiB. 300 MiB + 1 is refused by wrangler before it connects and by bex with `100100` (nothing stored).
- **Step 3.** The streamed PUT with a signed `Content-Length` from a network reader is accepted by Wasabi, which holds exactly the bytes sent (314,572,800 for the 300 MiB object, read back with the root credential). The fallback is not needed.
- **Step 4.** wrangler into `render-oregon` is read by the unmodified pinned `render ea objects` and by `bex ea objects`, and the other way round, byte for byte; `render-frankfurt` too.
- **Step 5.** ADR112 § Not verified, ADR018's Cloudflare row and `docs/bex-cli.md` updated, only for what ran.
- **Also found and fixed in the docs.** `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` was documented as "a bex API key"; a key's secret answers `10002` and the 15-minute `access_token` of a `client_credentials` exchange (or a human OAuth token) works. The "8 concurrent streams per workspace" is counted per bex-api replica (40 simultaneous puts: 16 accepted, 24 `429` `10058`).
- **Sweep, lifecycle and meters.** The hourly sweep's per-bucket usage equals Wasabi's byte for byte; a `Date` lifecycle rule deleted exactly its objects; `object_storage_gb_seconds`, `_ingress_bytes` and `_egress_bytes` appear in `GET /v1/usage` with no price, the estimate and `billing.currentCost` unchanged, Stripe untouched.
- **Scopes and isolation.** A read-only human token cannot get an object or write; a key bound to one workspace and a non-member id are refused `10003`; a bad bearer is `10002`.
- **Not run.** A workspace delete's purge (`createWorkspace` answers `PAYMENT_REQUIRED` even for the default plan; deleting a QA workspace would delete the QA data; covered by t010's real-Postgres tests), an `Age` expiry, the 10,000-deletion and quota caps. The race that [w4/678](../../../../w4/done/678.md) files (a deleted `render-<region>` is recreated under a new id) is not part of this walk.
- **Cleaned up.** Every key (the Wasabi listing of both buckets is empty), the three buckets, the API key (deleted), the three tokens (revoked, 401), the Kratos sessions (logged out), the kubeconfig and the scratch files. Left behind: the `usage_hourly` rows the meters wrote.
