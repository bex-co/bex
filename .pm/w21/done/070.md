# 070 — The SNI proxies count the global connection cap before the PROXY header and SNI are read, and nothing alerts on rejected connections

Why: one 1024-slot global cap taken at accept lets 8 IPv4 sources (or one IPv6 /64 before w21/m394 t017) hold every slot for the 10 s handshake deadline, a full outage of Postgres and Key Value over TLS needs about 3,072 connections across the three platform pods, and no alert watches `connections_rejected_total`, so it would be silent. ADR066 accepts this as the "edge/LB rate limiting" residual, but the Hetzner load balancer has no L4 limiter (ADR110 amends ADR066).

- **Source:** w21/m394 round 25, finding R25-12 (accepted residual; mitigation 1, the IPv6 /64 key, shipped in t017), 2026-11-14. Reproduced on the real `pg-sni-proxy`: a legitimate client served 25 of 25 before and 1 of 74 during the hold.
- **Size:** about 1h30.

## Steps

1. A separate per-source cap that applies before SNI resolution, with a lower count than the post-SNI cap (the PROXY header is read first; the source is known then).
2. A 2 s deadline for the pre-header bytes (the PROXY preamble and first record), keeping the 10 s for the full handshake.
3. A Prometheus alert on a sustained non-zero rate of rejected connections by scope (`source`, `workspace`, `global`).
4. Tests for each against the real handler, as `TestStalledBackendSSLReplyReleasesTheSlots` does.

## Files

- lego/operator/internal/sniproxy/limiter.go, meter.go
- lego/operator/cmd/pg-sni-proxy/main.go, lego/operator/cmd/kv-sni-proxy/main.go
- deploy/gitops/base/prometheus.yaml (alert rule)

## Acceptance

- One /24 of IPv4 sources cannot hold every global slot; a stalled pre-header connection is closed in 2 s; the alert fires in a test.

## Done (2026-11-14)

- **What the note missed:** `deploy/gitops/base/prometheus.yaml`'s scrape keep list for both proxies named only `egress_bytes_total|healthy|process_start_time_seconds`, so `connections_rejected_total` was dropped before Prometheus saw it; an alert alone would have watched nothing. The keep lists now include it.
- **Handshake cap (step 1), per network, not per address:** `lego/operator/internal/sniproxy/limiter.go` `AcquireHandshake` counts a connection from its PROXY header until its SNI is resolved against its network, an IPv4 /24 or an IPv6 /64 (`BEX_PROXY_MAX_HANDSHAKES_PER_NETWORK`, `BEX_KV_PROXY_MAX_HANDSHAKES_PER_NETWORK`, default 32), and sheds the rest with `reason="handshake"`. A per-address count could not meet the acceptance: 256 addresses of a /24 at any cap above 3 hold more than 1,024 slots, and 3 refuses an app server's connection pool. One /24 now holds at most 32 of the 1,024; the 8 sources that held them all now need 32 networks. A per-pod global handshake cap was written and dropped: a full pool sheds a new client exactly as a full global cap does, only sooner.
- **Pre-header deadline (step 2):** the first bytes (the PROXY preamble and, for Postgres, the 8-byte peek; for Key Value, the first byte of the record) get `sniproxy.PreHeaderTimeout()` of 2 s, then the handshake has the 10 s from accept as before. An attacker who sends one byte and stalls still holds a slot for 10 s, which the per-network cap bounds; ADR110 says so.
- **Alert (step 3):** `DatastoreProxyShedding` (warning) on `rate(...connections_rejected_total[5m]) > 0` for 10 m, by proxy and reason. `promtool` 3.13.5 (run from the pinned image) checks the pack and the new `datastore_proxy_shedding_alerts_test.yml`; mutating the `for`, the window, the second proxy and the `> 0` each fails it, against a passing control.
- **Tests (step 4):** limiter units (per network, IPv4-mapped, IPv6 /64, one /24 against a 1,024 cap, release exactly once, disabled), and the real `handleConn` of both proxies: a silent connection is closed at the pre-header timeout, a client that speaks in time keeps the rest of the deadline, and 12 stalled handshakes from one /24 leave 4 held, 8 shed and metered while a client from another network is admitted. Each handler rule was mutated to prove its test. `-race` caught a data race on the shortened timeout in tests, so it is an atomic.
- **Left:** a flood from enough networks (32 per pod) still fills the global cap, now alerted; ADR110 and ADR066 say so.
