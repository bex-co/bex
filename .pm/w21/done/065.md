# Repeated Suspend→Resume runs on production lose most of their `service_suspended` and `service_resumed` events, past w5/m369's "never lost" bullet: 6 runs gave 4 of 12 events, 3 runs 30 s apart gave 4 of 6

Why: w5/m369's Definition of done bullet 3 says a quick Suspend→Resume always records its `service_suspended`/`service_resumed` pair so push, email and webhooks fire; on the production pin that carries m369's mark and kick, 8 of 12 events were missing after six runs and 2 of 6 after three runs spaced 30 s apart, while the service itself did suspend and resume every time.

**Severity:** minor (the action always happens; its feed rows, and so anything subscribed to them, do not). **Estimate:** ~90m (trace which pass drops the events ~45m, fix ~30m, a repeated-toggle test ~15m); likely shares its cause with [w21/064](064.md), so decide that note's candidate (a) first. **Kind:** residual of [w5/m369](../blocked/m369/README.md) bullet 3 (t003, `RecordServiceSuspensionRequested` and `deferStaleSuspension`), whose t008 production replay is still open. **Source:** live `/qa-find-bugs` pass 270, 2026-11-13 10:56–11:03 UTC, production `https://dashboard.bex.co` and `https://api.bex.co`, workspace `bex`, deploy pin `96925d2f8` ("pin platform images to 5de3d5bcce8e", 2026-11-13T09:41:40Z; carries m369's `f076e96e7`), filed to w21 per user direction. **Status:** inbox.

## Root cause (not traced beyond m369's own record; the deciding step was not probed)

- m369 t003 marks the suspension checkpoint after the Suspend's audit row (`apps.setSuspended`, `PGStore.RecordServiceSuspensionRequested`) and kicks the projector on Suspend and Resume; each observation carries `SpecReadAt` so a checkpoint changed after the pass read the spec keeps its flag for that pass (`deferStaleSuspension`, "one pass at most"). The m369 test list has `TestSuspensionMarkRecordsTheQuickPair` and a real-Postgres twin for one pair; none toggles repeatedly or spaces the calls past one pass.
- **Candidates, none checked:** (a) a kick that arrives while a pass is running is coalesced into it and the next pass is the 30 s resync, the same candidate as w21/064, which fits events landing 21 s to 33 s after the call; (b) a second Suspend overwrites the checkpoint mark before any pass has read the first, so one mark stands for two suspends; (c) the projector writes one lifecycle fact per pass and drops the rest of a window's transitions. The audit log (`/v1/owners/{id}/audit-logs`) was not read, so it is unknown whether the audit rows exist for the missing events.

## Fix (target behavior)

Every `POST …/suspend` and `POST …/resume` of a service records its `service_suspended` or `service_resumed` event within 10 s of the call, in call order, however the calls are spaced, so six runs 5 s apart give 12 events and three runs 30 s apart give 6. REST, GraphQL, MCP, webhooks, push and the dashboard read the same rows, so one change moves them all.

1. Read the audit rows and the projector's passes around a repeated toggle to choose between (a), (b) and (c); the answer goes in the Done record.
2. Fix it, and add a test that toggles a service several times with a pass running and asserts one event per call.

## Done 2026-11-13 (loopx w21)

**Cause.** Candidate (a) is out: [064](../blocked/064.md)'s probes show the watch and the pass project a CR change within about a second, so the projector is not slow. The loss is in how `service_suspended` and `service_resumed` are derived. They come from `RecordObservedServiceState` comparing what a pass samples (`spec.suspended`, the phase) with the event checkpoint, which holds one flag. Two actions closer together than one pass reach it as one sample, and w5/m369's mark (the Suspend's write of that one bit) made it worse: a Suspend's mark landing before any pass had recorded the previous Resume overwrote it, and `deferStaleSuspension` then discarded the pass's own, older read. A Suspend that arrives while the previous Resume is still waking a stopped service (the 30 s-apart shape) lost its event the same way, because the held Resume's wake never completed to report. The audit rows were complete because they are written per call and never sampled.

**Fix.** Every accepted Suspend and Resume is appended to a new table, `service_suspension_changes` (migration 0250), by `apps.setSuspended` after its audit row, for both verbs. `RecordObservedServiceState` replays the rows in order inside the checkpoint's own transaction, then applies the pass's observation (`replaySuspensionChanges`, `lego/backend/internal/store/event_facts.go`; both the Postgres store and the test mirror call it). Each action goes through the same edge rules a pass sampling right after it would have used, against the checkpoint's own phase, so every earlier rule (w4/508, 527, 555, 562, m230) is unchanged, and a quick pair on a service that stayed up is dated at the two actions. Two additions, both from the model test below:

- **A Resume of a stopped service is held**, because its wake reports `service_resumed` at Ready (w4/562). A Suspend that arrives before that wake finishes ends it, so the held Resume is recorded then (as a Resume of a service that stays asleep, the existing rule) and the Suspend after it. The held row stays logged while the checkpoint has the service stopped.
- **A wake the pass itself reports** (checkpoint Hibernated, the pass sees Running) replays at its Ready time, or after the last logged Resume when there is none, between the actions around it. Without that a wake that had completed was reported twice, once by the pass and once as a superseded Resume.
- The pass's own flag gives way to the log's last one when an action is newer than the pass's read of the spec. The m369 mark and `RecordServiceSuspensionRequested` are gone.

**Tests.**

- `TestSuspensionLogReportsEveryAction` and `TestPGSuspensionLogReportsEveryAction` (the same cases on the in-memory store and on real Postgres): a Suspend undone before any pass, a pass that read before the Suspend, a long suspension, a sleeping service, six runs 5 s apart reaching one pass as six pairs, passes interleaved with the actions, a service that stops between them, a held Resume reported once at its wake, and four passes at once reporting each action once. With the replay switched off, 7 of the 9 cases fail (the other two guard the stale-read and long-suspension branches).
- `TestSuspensionReplayReportsEveryActionUnderAnyInterleaving` plays 20 000 random series (1–10 actions 1–30 s apart, an operator that stops and wakes the instance, 3–22 passes at random times that read the spec up to 6 s before they record, half of them with no Ready time) and asserts `service_suspended` and `service_resumed` alternate once per action. It found four holes in the first design, all fixed and kept as seeds: a wake reported twice, a pass's stale flag written back over a held Resume, the same flag restored by `deferStaleSuspension` after the log had corrected it, and a wake with no Ready time reported twice. 100 000 seeds pass.
- `TestSuspendAndResumeLogEachActionAndKickTheProjector` (the verbs log both actions and kick), `TestReplayWithNothingLoggedAppliesThePassAlone` (a pass that finds the rows another pass already replayed while it waited for the checkpoint's lock; the real-Postgres run hit this as a panic before it was guarded).
- Store, apps and events packages pass against the CI-pinned Postgres and OpenFGA on a fresh database.

**Cost.** The steady state adds no round trip: whether rows are pending rides in the checkpoint's existing `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, and the guarded `UPDATE` is skipped when nothing changed (one statement fewer than before). A pass with rows pending reads them and deletes them in the same transaction.

**Not done, and what a production check must expect.**

- **Not run on production.** It needs the deploy; the Definition of done's bullets are the checks.
- **Timing of `service_resumed` for a stopped service is by design Ready, not the call.** The note's "within 10 s of its call" holds for a Resume that does not wake a stopped service, and for one a Suspend supersedes (recorded then). A Resume that does wake one reports when the instance is Ready, which can be 10–30 s after the call (w4/562). Count and order are what the replay guarantees: six runs 5 s apart and three runs 30 s apart should read 6+6 and 3+3 in call order.
- **The operator's lag (w21/064) still delays the phase edges** (`service_hibernated`-side facts), not the counts.
- **Webhook and push delivery** read the same fact rows and were not exercised; neither was a paid plan or a service with two instances. The model does not cover a service that sleeps from idleness, or a spec flag changed by anything but these two verbs (those still come from sampling, as before).
- The log holds a row per accepted action, including a repeat of the same action; rows are deleted when replayed, so the table holds only what no pass has reported yet, plus at most one held Resume per stopped service.
