# 088 — The hourly object-storage sweep walks workspaces one at a time

Why: the sweep lists every workspace's prefix to measure usage and run lifecycle rules, and a sequential walk will not finish inside its hour once the store has many workspaces.

- **Source:** the w21/m416 worker's report, 2026-11-15 (the sweep itself is verified on production: per-bucket usage equals Wasabi's byte for byte). Production has few workspaces with objects today, so this is about headroom, not a failure.

## Steps

1. Measure the sweep's wall time on production against the number of workspaces with buckets, and write the per-workspace cost down.
2. Bound the walk: run workspaces with a small worker pool (a fixed count, the way the other sweeps do), keep one workspace's listing sequential, and cut the pass off cleanly at the interval so the next hour does not overlap.
3. Add a test with a fake store that proves the pool never exceeds its bound and a slow workspace does not hold the others.

## Done when

- [x] The sweep's duration is recorded and the walk is concurrent within a fixed bound, with a test.

## Result (2026-11-16)

Shipped in [971a79648](https://github.com/bex-co/bex/commit/971a79648) and [3c6654df6](https://github.com/bex-co/bex/commit/3c6654df6), both deployed and checked on production.

- **Walk.** `Sweeper.Pass` sweeps 4 workspaces at once (`sweepWorkers`, the `errgroup.SetLimit` shape of `core.PatchEach`); one workspace's regions and pages stay sequential, so 4 is also the most list requests in flight to Wasabi. A panic fails one workspace, not the process. `Run` ends each pass at the interval, a tick inside a pass is skipped, and a cut-off pass reports "n of m workspaces not reached" and leaves them with the oldest observations, which the next pass starts with (before, it listed in id order and the tail starved).
- **Duration.** The sweep logged nothing on success. It now logs `pass over N workspaces took T with 4 workers: ...` and exports `bex_usage_object_storage_sweep_*` (stored by Prometheus; the first names, `bex_object_storage_sweep_*`, were dropped by the bex-api scrape keep-list and were renamed in the second commit with a test that reads the keep-list).
- **Production.** 3 workspaces swept in 3.476 s (a replacement pod's first pass, with 2,500 objects in 3 pages under `bex-canary`, usage equal to the root listing byte for byte) and in 1.726 s at the natural hourly tick, 0 failed, 0 not reached. About 1.3 s a workspace; a 1,000-key page about 0.8 s from the cluster to Oregon. The numbers, the reason for 4 and the Wasabi request-limit facts are in [ADR112 D8](../../../docs/ADR112-object-storage.md).
- **Tests** (a fake store, no database): the pool never exceeds its bound, a slow workspace does not hold the others, a panic is contained, a cut-off pass records what it finished and the next pass starts with the rest, `Run` cuts each pass at its interval without overlap, and every metric is kept by the scrape job. Each fails with its fix removed.
- **Filed:** [095](../095.md) (does Wasabi count the listing's own bytes as egress: about 155 GB a month for one workspace at the object cap), [096](096.md) (no alert when the sweep stops completing).
