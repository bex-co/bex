# A forward `GET /v1/logs` chain silently drops lines when the window holds more than one instance's stream: `limit=100` returned 165 of 255 lines and ended `hasMore:false` (backward and single-stream forward are complete)

Why: Render documents the cursor chain (feed `nextStartTime`/`nextEndTime` back) as walking every line of a window, and [w4/m107](../w4/done/m107/README.md)'s definition of done requires "every line of the window exactly once"; a forward reader (`bex logs --direction forward`, an export script, an agent) loses a third of the lines of any service that redeployed or ran two instances in the window, and the last page says there is nothing more.

**Severity:** major (silent data loss on a documented API contract; no error, no `hasMore` signal). **Estimate:** ~90m (pin the cause at the Loki layer ~30m, fix ~40m, tests and sibling log types ~20m); promote to a milestone if the cause needs more than the per-stream read below. **Kind:** the m107 DoD bullet 3 guarantee does not hold for windows with more than one stream; m107's check used a single-stream service. **Source:** live `/qa-find-bugs` pass r200, 2026-11-12 16:5x–17:1x UTC, production `https://dashboard.bex.co` and `https://api.bex.co`, workspace `bex`, deploy pin `61a06de63` ("pin platform images to 77531cc764d3", 2026-11-12T16:40:49Z), found while verifying [w8/290](../w8/done/290.md) and filed to w21 per user direction. **Status:** inbox.

## Root cause (partly code-traced; the deciding step was not probed)

- A label-only forward read is one Loki `query_range` over the whole window with `limit`, `direction=forward` (`lego/backend/internal/logs/loki.go:79-97`; `sliced` is false without a line filter, `:109-117`, `:187-189`). `parseLokiStreams` sorts every stream's values together and `capToLimit` keeps the oldest `limit` (`loki.go:698-726`, `service.go:617-637`). Both are correct for whatever Loki returns, so the omitted `…2ff2` lines were **not in Loki's answer** to the two-stream forward query. This is inferred from the bex-api output; Loki itself was not queried this run (no cluster access).
- The cursor is the page's last timestamp (observed: `nextStartTime` equals the last returned line, `render.go` `pageCursors`, not re-read this run), so the lines before it that the page skipped can never be reached by the chain.
- Why Loki returns them partially (per-stream truncation, chunk batching or ingester/store merge) is the open question and is task 1 below.

## Fix (target behavior)

A forward chain (`startTime=<nextStartTime>`, `endTime=<nextEndTime>`) returns every line of the window exactly once, whatever number of instance streams the window holds, at any `limit`; `hasMore:false` means there is nothing left.

1. Pin the mechanism: run the same selector (`lokiQueryFor(namespace, q)`) against Loki with `direction=forward&limit=100` over probe 1's window and compare per-stream value counts with the bex-api page.
2. If Loki returns partial per-stream results for multi-stream forward reads, read forward pages stream by stream (one `query_range` per instance stream, each contiguous as probe 4 shows), merge, and cap in Go. Otherwise fix the mistake in our merge. REST, GraphQL and MCP share `Service.QueryLogs` and `NewLokiSource`, so one change moves all three.
3. Tests: a two-stream history source whose forward chain at limits 100 and 50 collects every line once, failing on the current code if the cause is on our side (otherwise an integration case against Loki).

**Done 2026-11-13:** the loss is Loki's, not bex's merge, and it is wider than this note said: the **backward** direction loses lines too. `NewLokiSource` no longer trusts a full page.

**Cause, reproduced locally (the deciding step this note could not take).** A Loki query frontend cuts a read at `split_queries_by_interval` (15m in the chart `deploy/gitops/base/loki.yaml` pins, rendered with `helm template` from its digest; 1h in Loki's own default) and merges the pieces (`mergeOrderedNonOverlappingStreams`, `pkg/querier/queryrange/codec.go`, v3.6.7). Two instance streams shaped like probe 1 (164 and 89 lines, the window crossing a cut), pushed to Loki 3.6.7 in containers: with the production limits, a raw forward `limit=100` returned 65 lines of the old instance and 35 of the new one (the live probe: 66 and 34), the forward chain returned 161 of 253 lines at `limit=100` and 210 of 253 at `limit=50` (live: 165 of 255, 214 of 255). The CI-pinned stock Loki gives the same numbers when the window crosses the top of an hour. Controls: a window inside one split interval is complete; the same window with `split_queries_by_interval: 0` is complete; a read of one stream across the cut is complete. A page of fewer than `limit` lines is exact, because the frontend returns the pieces whole when they total no more than `limit` (that branch is in the source). **Backward is affected, which probe 3 missed:** when the newest piece holds fewer than `limit` lines, a backward `limit=100` page held 5 wrong lines, and 9 of 45 random backward chains differed from the truth (forward: 8 of 45). Probe 3's shape had a newest piece of more than `limit` lines. I searched for an upstream Loki report and found none; that is a thin search, not proof it is unknown.

**Fix.** `lego/backend/internal/logs/loki_streams.go` (`lokiReader`, which `NewLokiSource` now uses): a page short of `limit` costs one request as before. A full page is read again stream by stream: the page's far line bounds the window, the streams in it come from `/loki/api/v1/series`, each is read alone with the query's own selector plus an equality matcher per label (an empty value where it has none), and the oldest or newest `limit` of the union is the page. With one stream in that window the page already is its own oldest or newest lines and is kept. A failed listing fails the read, a window of more than 256 streams answers `QUERY_TIMEOUT`, and at most 32 stream reads run at once across requests. Every log type and the raw pages of line-filtered scans share `NewLokiSource` (the only history reader, `cmd/api/main.go`), so request, build and datastore logs are covered by construction. `docs/ADR010-observability.md` records the rule. Not chosen: `split_queries_by_interval: 0` in `loki.yaml` removes the cause, but it also changes how Grafana and the request metrics read long ranges on a 1 Gi single-binary Loki, which I could not evaluate here.

**Tests.** `loki_streams_test.go` (a fake Loki that merges the way the frontend does): forward and backward full pages are the right lines, one request for a short page, selector pinning with an extra `app_id` label, the listing's window bound in each direction, a failed listing, the stream cap, shards, a caller leaving mid-read, and a line-filtered scan whose match sits where the inexact page dropped it; with the re-read disabled 10 of the 13 fail (the others are the one-request guard for a short page and two selector-construction tests that do not go through a read). `loki_split_test.go` runs the chains on the real Loki (`BEX_TEST_LOKI_URL`) across the last top of the hour: with the re-read disabled they return 161 of 253 (limit 100), 211 of 253 (limit 50) and 204 of 213 (backward), and with it all three are complete. Three fakes in the existing tests (`loki_test.go` twice, `exclusiveEndLoki`) now answer the series listing, because a full page triggers it. Random shapes (1 to 5 streams, random starts, steps and lengths, unique stamps, the window across a cut, limits 7, 50 and 100, 15 trials each): the per-stream algorithm, with and without the one-stream shortcut, matched the truth in all 45 chains per direction.

**Not done, and not known.** The production checks of this note's Definition of done (the forward chain at limits 100 and 50 equal to the 30 s-window read, the backward chain, and request and build logs with several overlapping streams) need the deploy. The cost: a full page now adds one series request, plus one read per stream when the window holds more than one. A limit cut inside a group of lines that share one nanosecond across streams was not examined (the fuzz's first run shared instants deliberately; its naive backward chain lacks bex's `capToLimit` group rule, so it says nothing about bex there).
