# 114 — One S3 upload stream to `oregon` runs at about 1 MiB/s, so a single `PutObject` over about 1.7 GiB cannot finish inside the 30-minute stream deadline

Why: the production walk of w21/m500 t001 measured it, and ADR112 D3 promises streams "up to 5 GiB for up to 30 minutes" without saying that this holds only where the stream is fast; `aws s3 cp` (multipart above 8 MiB) is not affected, but an SDK `PutObject` of a large body is.

- **Source:** w21/m500 t001, 2026-11-19 (ADR112 § Not verified, "Streams").
- **Facts:** 16 concurrent 256 MiB `put-object` calls to `oregon` averaged 0.94 MiB/s each (271 s), from a laptop; the same single stream from a pod in the cluster was equally slow (the serving pod used 8 to 27 millicores, where the Frankfurt stream used 408). The same 5 GiB `PutObject` to `frankfurt` took 320 s (16.8 MiB/s, the pace of one workspace on one replica) and read back identical. So the slow hop is `bex-s3gw` to Wasabi in Oregon from the Hetzner nodes (a long-latency path per connection), not the client or the pacing. At 1 MiB/s the 30-minute deadline ends a stream at about 1.7 GiB.
- **Not yet known:** whether the cause is the TCP window of the Wasabi connection (socket buffers, the transport's settings in `internal/objectstorage` streaming), a per-connection limit at Wasabi, or the route; a single-stream `curl` PUT from a node to the Wasabi `us-west-1` endpoint, with and without larger socket buffers, tells which. Render's own object storage limits are unpublished, so no Render number applies.
- **Fix:** if buffers explain it, set them (and record the new rate); if not, document the limit where `bex s3` and the S3 section of `docs/bex-cli.md` say "5 GiB per object": a body over about 1 GiB to `oregon` needs multipart (which every aws-cli, rclone and boto3 transfer uses by default), and `bex s3`'s docs say so. Keep the 5 GiB cap either way.

## Done when

- [x] The slow hop is identified with a measurement, and a single 1 GiB `PutObject` to `oregon` either runs at the pace of one workspace (16 MiB/s) or `docs/bex-cli.md` and ADR112 D3 state the length a single stream to `oregon` can complete.

## Resolved 2026-11-19: the 1 MiB/s did not reproduce, and no hop is slow

Measured on production (pin `98e053468f52`, `bex-s3gw` image `sha256:1ec4a7c2…`) from about 23:00 UTC; the full figures, method and cleanup are in [ADR112 § Not verified](../../../docs/ADR112-object-storage.md#not-verified), "A single stream to `oregon`". In short:

- **Node to Wasabi, no bex in the path.** One TCP connection from a pod on each platform node (round trip 152 to 164 ms to `us-west-1`) PUT 128 MiB at 14.1 to 14.8 MiB/s, and 512 MiB at 16.2 MiB/s (`frankfurt`: 43 to 61 MiB/s). `ss -ti` showed almost no loss, `rwnd_limited` 72 to 79% of the time against Wasabi's advertised window of about 3.1 MB (about 18 MiB/s at 160 ms) and `sndbuf_limited` only 8 to 12%: one connection to Oregon is capped by Wasabi's receive window near 17 MiB/s, which is the 16 MiB/s pace, so socket buffers on bex's side would change nothing.
- **Through `objects.onbex.co`.** aws-cli 2.39.5's default `put-object` of 256 MiB: 14.7 MiB/s to `oregon`, 15.9 to `frankfurt`. **One 2 GiB `put-object` to `oregon`: 133.8 s, 15.3 MiB/s**, size read back as sent. 16 concurrent 64 MiB puts: 2.0 MiB/s each in both regions (the pace is shared by a workspace's streams on a replica). From the laptop: 9.1 and 4.6 MiB/s to `oregon`, 5.8 to `frankfurt`.
- **Why the walk read 1 MiB/s.** Not established. Its laptop figures (1 GiB multipart, 16 streams) were both about 15 MiB/s in all, which fits the laptop's own link; the in-cluster single-stream reading this note quotes is in none of the walk's records. Not run: the measure at the walk's hour (about 11:00 UTC); if a slow reading recurs, `ss -ti` on the gateway's node separates the hops.
- **Landed.** `bex s3`'s limits paragraph in `docs/bex-cli.md`, ADR112 D3 and ADR018's S3 row now say the pace holds to either region, that 5 GiB takes about 5.5 minutes, and that a link under about 3 MiB/s (or several large uploads at once in one workspace) belongs in multipart; `TestPacesLetAMaximumObjectFinishInsideTheStreamDeadline` fails if either pace drops under twice what a 5 GiB object needs in 30 minutes (a 1 MiB/s pace fails it). The 5 GiB cap is unchanged.
