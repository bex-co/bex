## Objective

Bound how many tenant-registry reads can run at once per registry host and per workspace, stop a failing host from consuming reads, and back off failures, so that tenant-controlled network waits cannot monopolise the operator's two App reconcile workers.

## Context

- Finding R25-04 (Low). `lego/operator/internal/controller/app_controller.go:742` (in `Reconcile`, declared at `:611`) calls `resolveImageNetwork` synchronously; `ReadImageConfig` (`image_network.go:256`) is bounded at 5 s (`httpclient.RequestTimeout`, dial 2 s, TLS 2 s, response header 3 s); a transient failure requeues after `min(max(Retry-After, 30 s), 5 min)` (`:613`, in `imageRegistryWait` `:602`) and failures are never cached; production runs 2 workers (`BEX_APP_RECONCILE_WORKERS`, `lego/operator/config/manager/manager.yaml:181-182`). Simulation (`sim.py`, not a live measurement): the workers are fully busy from about 14 attacker apps; the mean wait for another tenant's reconcile is about 29 s at 25 attacker apps, 93 s at 50, 278 s at 125. A Hobby workspace holds 25 services and a user 5 workspaces (`plans.go:77`).
- Verifier correction: a single global non-blocking semaphore of size workers-1 is ONE slot shared by every host and tenant and could be held nearly continuously by 14 or more tarpit apps, moving the starvation to other tenants' image deploys. Cap per registry host and per workspace, add a per-host failure breaker (`activityBreaker` in `lego/operator/internal/controller/activity.go`, 30 s, is a precedent), and keep legitimate Docker Hub, GHCR and Zot reads out of a failing host's slot.
- Precedents: ADR068 #6 fixed the same worker-occupancy hazard on the publish path; ADR039 O-10 capped the read at 5 s (this is its residual). The queue already backed up in production (w4/603, w21/012); `BexOperatorReconcileQueueSlow` fires at p95 over 15 s for 10 minutes.

## Steps

1. Write the failing test first in `lego/operator/internal/controller/image_network_test.go`: concurrent `resolveImageNetwork` calls against tarpit registries (one host, then many hosts of one workspace) with two workers configured must leave capacity for an unrelated host and workspace (today every call enters the tarpit and holds a worker 5 s).
2. Add a non-blocking per-host and per-workspace limit around the external read that returns a transient busy result (a quick requeue) when full, and a per-host failure breaker; replace the flat 30 s requeue with per-App exponential backoff on consecutive transient failures; optionally shrink the tenant-host read budget to 2-3 s.
3. Check that Docker Hub, GHCR and the platform Zot registry are unaffected by a failing host (test).
4. Run `make test` for the operator and `make lint`.

## Result (2026-11-14)

- **Design.** The finding's verifier warned that one global slot of workers-1 would only move the starvation to other tenants' image deploys, so there is no global slot. `internal/controller/registry_read_gate.go` adds `registryReadGate`: one read at a time per registry host and per workspace, a breaker per host that closes it for 30 s after a failed read and doubles to 5 minutes on consecutive failures (an answer, even a refusal, resets it), and a `registryBusyError` in place of waiting whenever a bound or the breaker says no, so the reconcile worker is freed at once. `resolveImageNetwork` reads a tenant-named registry (Docker Hub, GHCR and any host the tenant names) through `readExternalImageConfig`; the read of the platform's own registry (`r.Registry`) is not tenant-controlled and is not gated. Each host has its own state, so a failing host cannot touch Docker Hub, GHCR or Zot.
- **Requeue.** `imageRegistryWait` now backs off per App: consecutive transient failures requeue after 30 s, 60 s, 120 s, 240 s, then 5 minutes (a registry's own `Retry-After` still wins inside that range, and the first failure is the old flat 30 s), reset by a successful read or the App's deletion. A busy answer requeues in 5 to 10 s (or the breaker's remaining window plus up to 5 s) and does not count as a failure. Both set the same Deploying "waiting for the image registry" status as before.
- **Bounds.** The gate forgets a failing host 5 minutes after its breaker window once it holds 512 hosts, so tenant-chosen host names cannot grow it without bound (tested with 2000 hosts); the per-App counters are cleared on success and on deletion.
- **Tests** (`registry_read_gate_test.go`, `-race`): the gate's per-host and per-workspace caps, the breaker's 30 s / 60 s / 5 min windows and reset, the sweep, and the per-App delays 30/60/120/240/300/300; through the real `resolveImageNetwork` with a transport that holds every read of a tarpit host: 10 attacker Apps over 2 hosts in one workspace leave at most 1 read in the tarpit, 8 or more come back as an immediate busy answer, and an unrelated tenant's read answers in under a second while the tarpit is held (before: every call sat in the tarpit); 6 Apps on 6 hosts of one workspace hold 1; a failing host's next read does not reach it, and a healthy host is unaffected; `imageRegistryWait` doubling, the busy answer, and the reset. With the gate's three refusals disabled, five of these fail. `registry.NewTransientError` is a new exported constructor, used by the test.
- **Suites.** `make lint` exit 0 and `GOWORK=off make test` exit 0 (the controller envtest suite, 335 s); after the last lint-only edits (`errors.AsType`, preallocation, removing an alias) the image, registry and port tests were re-run with `-race`. Main's `go lint` was red when this landed from three other workstreams' tests (`predeploy_test.go` complexity 31, a `newexpr` and a `prealloc` finding); those were fixed and shipped first, alone, as `c99e7815e` `fix(ci)`.
- **For t005's ADR109.** The read budget stays 5 s (the finding's optional 2-3 s for tenant hosts was not taken: a slow but honest registry, such as a cold Docker Hub token fetch, would start failing). An attacker holding several hosts across several workspaces can still occupy both workers for the first rounds: each host costs one 5 s read, then its breaker closes it for 30 s, doubling to 5 minutes, so the occupancy decays to about 2% of a worker per host. Moving the read out of the worker stays out of scope.
