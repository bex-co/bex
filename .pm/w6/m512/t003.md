## Objective

`reconcileKeyValueInternalProxy` sets `BEX_KV_DRAIN_SECONDS` and a `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` that covers it, in the same template write as the image change, so no store rolls twice. A drain must not let a store's tenants take Valkey's platform reserve of 10.

## Context

- `lego/operator/internal/controller/keyvalue_internal_proxy.go` builds the template.
- w6/m469's hold keeps the image while the binary is byte-identical. This milestone changes the binary, so the env and grace period ride on the roll the binary already causes.
- ADR021 § 9: Valkey runs `--maxclients` at the plan's limit plus 10, and each proxy pod's budget is the plan's limit.
- `deploymentRolloutReady` (`app_controller.go`) counts no terminating pod, so a draining old pod does not hold the store's readiness.

## Steps

1. Choose the bound and justify it in ADR021 (t006). Weigh in-flight commands, how long the reserve is exposed, and node drains, which respect the grace period. Short commands finish in milliseconds, while a blocking command waits as long as its client asked. Something in the 30–60 s range is the expected answer. Render documents no figure for Key Value.
2. Set the env and `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`. Make the grace period the drain plus a few seconds' margin, so the process closes its streams itself before `SIGKILL`.
3. Measure the reserve risk and bound it. While an old pod drains, the old and new pods together can admit up to twice the limit. Find whether a client can actually push the upstream past `--maxclients` during a drain (pools keep their old connections, so growth needs new dials). Then fix it. Options:
   - make the new pod's `--check` treat Valkey's maxclients refusal as "up";
   - have a draining pod stop counting toward the budget;
   - shorten the drain.
   Record the choice in t006.
