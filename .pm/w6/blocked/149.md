# An external datastore stream silent for an hour, and every stream at 24 h, is closed without notice

Why: Render documents no close of an idle or long-lived external Key Value or Postgres connection, and ADR018 records bex's 1 h idle and 24 h lifetime closes as a divergence with no sign-off ([ADR018 § Other datastores & storage](../../../docs/ADR018-render-parity.md#other-datastores--storage)).

- **Filed by** w6/148 (2026-11-20), which made the SNI proxies' idle bound count bytes in either direction, so a subscriber that only receives stays open while its messages arrive. What still closes, through `bex-kv-sni-proxy` and `bex-pg-sni-proxy` alike: a `SUBSCRIBE` or `LISTEN` client that gets no message for 1 h, a pooled connection or an interactive `psql` session silent for 1 h, and any stream 24 h after it was routed (`BEX_PROXY_IDLE_TIMEOUT` / `BEX_KV_PROXY_IDLE_TIMEOUT`, default `1h`; `BEX_PROXY_MAX_LIFETIME` / `BEX_KV_PROXY_MAX_LIFETIME`, default `24h`; `lego/operator/cmd/{pg,kv}-sni-proxy/main.go`, `sniproxy.CopyBidirectional`). Valkey's own `timeout` is `0` by default and "does not apply to Pub/Sub clients" ([valkey.io/topics/clients](https://valkey.io/topics/clients/)), and PostgreSQL closes no idle session by default.
- **Why the bounds exist.** F6 of the 2026-08-16 repository security review ([w1/m65](../../w1/done/m65/README.md) t008): a routed copy could idle forever after auth, and the proxies had no admission caps then. Since w6/m457 every route admits at most twice its datastore's limit and each process 4,096 streams ([ADR009 § External connection admission](../../../docs/ADR009-postgresql-management.md#external-connection-admission-w6m457-2026-11-17)), so an idle stream holds a slot its datastore's own limit already counts. ADR008 § Render parity first says to amend that hardening choice, not keep it.
- **Weigh before building.** The parity-sufficient choice is likely no idle bound and no lifetime on a routed stream, as on Valkey and PostgreSQL, with dead peers still reaped. Go enables TCP keepalive (15 s) on both proxy sockets by default (`net.Listen`, `net.DialTimeout`). Check first:
  1. Behind the edge LB (PROXY protocol), the client socket's keepalive probes the LB's leg, not the end client. Find what the Hetzner LB does with an idle TCP flow and with a client that vanished.
  2. The drain bound is tied to the idle timeout (`TestTheDaemonSetGivesTheDrainItsBound`, [ADR021 § 10](../../../docs/ADR021-keyvalue-management.md)). Without an idle bound it needs a rule of its own.
  3. Memory: about 95 KB per idle stream, 4,096 per process (ADR009).
- **End state.** Through both proxies on production, an external subscriber that gets no message for more than 1 h, an idle `psql` session, and a stream older than 24 h stay open, and a client that vanished is still closed within minutes. ADR009, ADR021, ADR018 and `lego/operator/AGENTS.md`'s SNI rows say so. A stricter variant is its own item. A user sign-off recorded in ADR018 would close this item instead.

## Progress — 2026-11-20 (the proxies' bounds are gone in `9ea476b3b`; the edge load balancer's 1 h idle close needs the user)

- **Verdict: work, then blocked.** At `9e792160a` both proxies defaulted to a 1 h idle and a 24 h lifetime bound (`defaultIdleTimeout`, `defaultMaxLifetime` in `cmd/{pg,kv}-sni-proxy/main.go`), and neither DaemonSet set the variables. The checks below found a second closer in front of them: the Hetzner edge load balancer closes a flow that carries no data for an hour, and nothing in the proxies can change that.
- **1. The edge load balancer, measured on production (pin `a47204c7e`).** All probes ran in a scratch namespace, `w6-149-probe`, on `bex-tenant-0-wldb9-4pxsp`, with tcpdump in the pod before netfilter.
  - **Key Value listener.** Two TLS clients through `red-dbpf5q5me2kc73f2l0og.kv.bex.co:6379` went silent after `AUTH` and `CLIENT SETNAME`. 5 s later the pod dropped every packet of the second in its own network namespace (iptables, `NET_ADMIN`), as a client that vanishes does. For the hour the LB (`49.12.20.236`) sent neither a packet: no keepalive probe, no close. Valkey's `CLIENT LIST`, read every 60 s on a new connection, held both until 07:56:25Z, exactly 1 h after their last bytes, when both closed in the same second and the LB sent each a FIN (retransmitting the vanished one's on backoff). The proxies' 1 h bound and the LB's fire at the same moment, so this alone cannot say which closed them.
  - **HTTP listener.** The LB's `:80` is a TCP service with PROXY protocol too, in front of Traefik `web`, whose read deadline is 6,000 s (`respondingTimeouts.readTimeout`; Traefik v3 sets that deadline on each accepted connection and keeps it through its first-byte peek, and sends a TCP keepalive every 3 min). Two raw TCP connections opened at 07:14:19.346Z and sent nothing, the second vanishing as above. The LB sent each a FIN at 08:14:19.346Z, exactly 3,600 s later. Control: the same silent connection straight to each Traefik pod's `web` port (`:8000`), bypassing the LB, opened at 08:28:49Z and was still open at 09:30:13Z, past the hour.
  - **So** the LB closes a flow that has carried no data for 1 h, whatever the backend's deadline, and Traefik's keepalive probes on its leg do not hold it. Hetzner exposes no idle setting on a TCP service (`http.timeout_idle` is HTTP only). A flow that carries data stays: w6/m515 t006's Postgres client, one query every 10 min, held one connection for 6 h. A vanished client is reaped at that same hour, so removing the proxies' bound leaves no dead stream behind.
- **2. The drain.** Its bound no longer follows the proxies' idle timeout. `TestTheDaemonSetGivesTheDrainItsBound` keeps it at most an hour (the LB closes an idle external stream within it anyway, and a stream that never ends is closed once whatever the bound), and at most a set idle timeout. Both DaemonSets keep `3600`.
- **3. Memory.** The 4,096-stream cap bounds it however long a stream idles: 412 MB measured at 4,096 idle routed streams, under the 512 Mi limit (ADR009).
- **Shipped** as `9ea476b3b`. `sniproxy.StreamBounds` reads both proxies' bounds, unset by default, and the variables stay as incident levers. `TestByDefaultASilentStreamStaysOpenUntilEitherSideCloses` holds an unbounded stream silent for 1 s, relays after it, and closes it when either side closes; with the old defaults it fails (`unset bounds = idle 1h0m0s, lifetime 24h0m0s`). A busy external stream now lives past 24 h. ADR009 § External connection admission, ADR021 § 9 and § 10, ADR018 § Other datastores & storage (still a divergence, now the LB's), the operator guide's SNI rows, `.env.example` and both DaemonSets' drain comments say so.
- **Rollout.** Both SNI binaries change, and `config/prod` renders byte-identical (only YAML comments changed), so nothing rolls at push. The next pin moves both SNI pins anyway, for `17e8848c0` and `9e792160a`, so this adds no roll. Its old pods run the current binary, so w6/m518 t006 reads the same drain.

## The decision (for `/unblock w6`)

An external Key Value or Postgres client that sends and receives nothing for an hour (a `SUBSCRIBE` with no messages, a `LISTEN` with no `NOTIFY`, an idle pool or `psql` session) is closed by the edge load balancer, where Render documents no such close. Which do you want?

1. **Take the datastore ports off the load balancer.** Clients reach the platform nodes' public addresses directly. The proxies would see the real client, so their own keepalive reaps a vanished one within about 2.5 min. It needs `hostPort` or host networking on `:5432` and `:6379` again (w6/m515 removed `hostPort` so a roll could surge), per-node DNS kept in step with node replacement, the cloud firewall opened on those ports, and the PROXY-protocol trust replaced. Clients lose the LB's health-checked failover. A milestone of its own.
2. **Ask Hetzner** to raise or expose the TCP idle timeout on the edge load balancer. Only the account owner can open that request.
3. **Sign off the 1 h idle close** in ADR018 as a recorded divergence, and close this item.
