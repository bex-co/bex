## Objective

Show on the local kind/CAPD cluster that the operator reads listeners through the reader on each worker and finds what the sweep found.

## Context

- `bash scripts/mock-cluster.sh` brings the cluster up, `bash scripts/mock-cluster.sh operator` builds and deploys `bex-operator:dev` (pre-approved, root `AGENTS.md`). Use this workstream's `dev-6` stack for bex-api (`bash scripts/dev-env.sh 6 up`).
- The local cluster runs Calico, not Cilium, so the conntrack table itself is measured only on production (t007). Locally, count connections instead: the operator's connects to a tenant pod show in the pod's node `conntrack -L` or in the operator's own sockets.

## Steps

1. Deploy the reader and the new operator. Check one reader pod per worker, Ready.
2. A web service whose process binds 8000 with `PORT` unset (`examples/` or `busybox httpd -p 0.0.0.0:8000`): it goes live on 8000 with `==> Detected HTTP on port 8000; serving it`.
3. A private service that binds 6379 and an undeclared high port such as 50051, plus a loopback-only listener: `openPorts` lists 6379 and 50051, never the loopback one. Open a new listener after readiness and see it published within the rescan interval.
4. The operator's log and counter show `source=reader` for each, no sweep. Stop the reader on one node (scale its DaemonSet off that node) and show the operator counts the no-reader fallback there; restore it.
5. Record each result here.

## Record (2026-11-20, local CAPD cluster, Calico, Linux 7.0.12-linuxkit arm64)

- **Deploy:** `bash scripts/mock-cluster.sh operator` built `bex-operator:dev` from the tree (exit 0). The reader is not in `config/default` yet (t002), so it was applied from `kubectl kustomize config/listener-reader`, with namespace `bex-system`, prefix `bex-` and image `bex-operator:dev`. Its pods were first refused: `violates PodSecurity "baseline:latest": host namespaces (hostPID=true)`, because the harness had stripped the namespace's `enforce: privileged` label (fixed in `scripts/mock-cluster.sh`, t002). With the declared labels restored, `kubectl -n bex-system get pods -l app.bex.co/component=listener-reader -o wide` showed one Ready reader on each untainted worker, `bex-tenant-0-48vl5-t29xw-p7plq` and `bex-worker-0-cg5cm-8m4qs-6m2tf`, from 19:17:28Z.
- **NetworkPolicy (Calico enforces it):** a busybox pod in `bex-system` labelled `control-plane=controller-manager` ran `wget "http://<reader>:9097/v1/listeners?pod=<uid>&ip=<ip>"` and got `{"ports":[8123,8126]}` for t001's `hostUsers: false` probe pod. The same pod labelled otherwise timed out.
- **Step 2:** App `w6-m530-web` (namespace `tea-dbtf3l1jg4ra1r0m4ur0`, dev-6's workspace) is a `web_service` on `busybox:1.36.1` with `startCommand: httpd -f -p 0.0.0.0:8000` and no port, so PORT is 10000. Its check of 10000 failed, two reads agreed, and at 19:20:53Z the manager logged `detected the port the process listens on` (`port: 8000`, `was: 10000`). `status.detectedPort` is 8000, and the App went `Running` on the next ReplicaSet with `Ready=True Deployed: 1/1 replicas ready`. The hint the stall message carried was `The process is listening on port 8000, which the service now uses.`.
- **Step 3:** App `w6-m530-pserv` is a `private_service` with port 6379 and `startCommand: httpd -p 0.0.0.0:50051; httpd -p 127.0.0.1:7000; httpd -f -p 0.0.0.0:6379`. Its `status.runtimePorts` was `{"ports":[6379,50051],"revision":"rev-1"}` at 19:20:19Z: the loopback-only 7000 was not found. `kubectl exec … -- httpd -p 0.0.0.0:51000` at 19:21:47Z was published at 19:22:22Z as `[6379,50051,51000]`, 35 s later and inside one rescan interval.
- **Step 4:** the manager's `/metrics` (port-forward to `:8080`) read `bex_port_listener_reads_total{source="reader",result="ok"} 5` and `{source="sweep",result="ok"} 4`. The 4 sweeps all ran before any reader pod existed: the log's `no listener reader on the pod's node; sweeping the pod's ports instead` lines are at 19:11:09Z and 19:15:12Z, for other workers' Apps on `bex-worker-0`. Two of them landed in one window, which showed the rate limit racing; it now uses `CompareAndSwap`. At 19:22:58Z a `NotIn bex-worker-0…` node affinity took the reader off that node. The manager logged one fallback line for that node at 19:23:14Z, and the counter rose to `sweep ok 7` (both private services there were swept, the record unchanged at `[6379,50051,51000]`) while `reader ok` reached 6 for the other node. The affinity was removed at 19:24:51Z and both readers were Ready again; a surge roll keeps a Ready reader on each node.
