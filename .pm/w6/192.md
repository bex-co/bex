# Accept Render's larger Postgres plans (`2c-8g` … `128c-1024g`) once bex can run them

Why: render.com/pricing sells 24 Postgres plans and bex has a rung for six. The other 18 had no board item until w4/m690's price guard gave every Render row a disposition, and a gap must name its owner.

- **Render (render.com/pricing § Compute > Postgres, read 2026-11-26; `lego/backend/internal/pricing/testdata/render-postgres-prices.tsv`):** `2c-8g` $100, `2c-16g` $160, `4c-16g` $200, `4c-32g` $350, `8c-32g` $400, `8c-64g` $750, `16c-64g` $800, `16c-128g` $1,500, `32c-128g` $1,700, `32c-256g` $2,500, `48c-192g` $2,500, `48c-384g` $4,500, `64c-256g` $3,000, `64c-512g` $6,000, `96c-384g` $4,600, `96-768g` (Render's spelling) $9,000, `128c-512g` $6,200 and `128c-1024g` $11,000 a month, with 200 connections at 8 GB, 400 at 16 GB and 500 from 32 GB.
- **bex today:** `tiers.yaml` stops at `basic-4gb` (`2c-4g`), and `postgresInputAliases` refuses these IDs by name ("plan must be one of …"). `price_table_test.go` marks each a gap owned by this note.
- **Gate, as for compute (`w6/blocked/114`, `w6/blocked/m332`):** node capacity. A database with 8 GB or more needs the `bex-tenant-xl` pool (cx53, out of stock in eu-central since 2026-11-12) and the 32 GB and larger plans dedicated vCPUs (the user declined raising the limit on 2026-11-16). Advertising a plan that cannot be scheduled is worse than refusing it. Expect to park this note on that gate.
- **Work once a pool fits:** a `tiers.yaml` rung and `connlimit.Postgres` limit per size that fits, the `pricing.yaml` rate at Render × 0.70 and its Stripe Price (`scripts/stripe-billing-setup.py`, `TestBillableMeterNamesArePinned`: the catalog is at Stripe's 20 items per Subscription), the aliases, the dashboard picker, and the row's disposition in `price_table_test.go` moved from gap to tier.
- **Done when:** each size bex can schedule is accepted on REST, GraphQL, MCP, Blueprints and the dashboard at Render's price less 30%, and the rest stay gaps with their gate written here.
