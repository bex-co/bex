# 178 — A background worker's old instance keeps running 60 seconds after its replacement starts, as Render's zero-downtime sequence does for workers

Why: Render runs its zero-downtime deploy sequence for background workers too, and its step 4 sends `SIGTERM` to the original instance only "After 60 seconds". bex's old worker gets `SIGTERM` as soon as the new one starts. The 60-second overlap was dropped by an engineering call in w1/m154, with no user sign-off recorded in ADR018.

- **Source:** `/pm-brainstorm one aspect of render parity to w6` 2026-11-24 (zero-downtime deploys and health checks), item 3, filed by `/pm them to w6`.
- **Render:** [render.com/docs/deploys](https://render.com/docs/deploys) (read 2026-11-24): "This zero-downtime deploy sequence applies to web services, private services, background workers, and cron jobs." Step 4: "After 60 seconds, Render sends a SIGTERM signal to your app's process on the original instance." This rests on that sentence; it has not been measured on Render.
- **bex:** `drainSecondsFor` returns 0 for a worker (`lego/operator/internal/controller/deployment_projection.go:658-667`: "A worker takes no inbound traffic, so it has nothing to drain"). w1/m154 exempted workers (`.pm/w1/done/m154/README.md`, "Exempt: no inbound traffic"). The `docs/ADR018/services-lifecycle.md` row "Graceful shutdown (`maxShutdownDelaySeconds`)" is ✅ and records no divergence.
- **Do:**
  - Give a worker without a disk the 60-second drain as a plain sleep. A worker has no health check, so `healthProbeDrainHandler` has nothing to run first.
  - Raise its termination grace period to the drain plus `maxShutdownDelaySeconds`, as for web services.
  - A disk-bearing worker keeps 0: it rolls with `Recreate`, and Render keeps its shutdown delay fixed.
- **Check:** a suspend and a scale-down of a worker then also wait 60 s before `SIGTERM`, as a web service's already do. Record that in ADR004 beside the w1/m154 drain.
- **Done when:**
  - envtest shows a worker Deployment's `preStop` sleeps 60 s and its grace period covers the drain plus the shutdown delay;
  - a deploy of a disposable worker in `bex-canary` logs its old instance's `SIGTERM` about 60 s after the new instance started;
  - the ADR018 Graceful shutdown row says so.
- **Size:** ~45m.

## Progress 2026-11-25 (loopx w6)

Shipped in `2d99cd7583`. Parked until production's pin carries it (deploys are batched every 6 hours).

- **Code:** `drainSecondsFor` (`lego/operator/internal/controller/deployment_projection.go`) gives every diskless App the 60 s drain, workers included. A worker's drain is the plain `preStop.sleep` (it has no health check, so `drainCheck` leaves it out and its pod carries no helper, installer or volume), and its grace period is 60 s plus `maxShutdownDelaySeconds`.
