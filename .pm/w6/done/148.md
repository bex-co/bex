# An external Key Value subscriber is disconnected every hour while its messages keep arriving

Why: a pub/sub client through `bex-kv-sni-proxy` (or a Postgres `LISTEN` client through `bex-pg-sni-proxy`) that only receives is closed after 1 h even while traffic flows to it, so every subscriber reconnects hourly and can lose the messages published while it is away. Valkey's own `timeout` "does not apply to Pub/Sub clients" ([valkey.io/topics/clients](https://valkey.io/topics/clients/), read 2026-11-20), and Render documents no such close.

- **Filed by** w6/m515 t006 (2026-11-20), from its production probe ([t006 § Step 2, the SNI-moving deploy](m515/done/t006.md#step-2-the-sni-moving-deploy-2026-11-20-pin-a47204c7e)). `m515-t006-kv-idle` in `bex-canary` ran `SUBSCRIBE m515:t006:idle` through the edge (`red-dbpf5q5me2kc73f2l0og.kv.bex.co:6379`) and received a message every 10 min. Its connections 1 to 5 each ended with `Server closed the connection` exactly 1 h after their `SUBSCRIBE` (2026-11-19 23:28:18Z, then 00:28:23Z, 01:28:28Z, 02:28:33Z, 03:28:38Z on 2026-11-20). No deploy or roll happened in that window. `m515-t006-pg-idle`, which sent a keepalive every 10 min, stayed on one connection for 6 h.
- **Cause.** The 1 h idle bound (`BEX_KV_PROXY_IDLE_TIMEOUT` / `BEX_PROXY_IDLE_TIMEOUT`, default `1h`, F6 from w1/m65) is applied to each direction alone. `CopyBidirectional` in `lego/operator/internal/sniproxy/meter.go` runs one `copyStream` per direction, each refreshes only its own source's read deadline, and the first to time out closes both. A subscriber sends nothing after `SUBSCRIBE`, so its client→backend read times out at 1 h whatever the backend sends.
- **End state.** A relayed stream counts as idle only when neither direction has moved a byte for the bound: bytes in either direction refresh both deadlines (or one shared last-activity time). The 24 h max-lifetime cap stays as it is. A test in `internal/sniproxy` holds a stream whose client sends nothing while the backend writes every fraction of the bound, and it stays open past the bound. A stream silent both ways still closes at the bound, and the existing idle tests still pass. `lego/operator/AGENTS.md`'s SNI rows and ADR021/ADR009 say what idle means.
- **Weigh before building.** A subscriber that gets no message for 1 h is still closed under that rule, where Valkey would keep it. Exempting it would need the proxy to parse RESP, which it does not (it relays TLS end to end). Record the remaining gap in ADR018 if it stays. In-cluster clients are not affected: the per-store `kv-internal-proxy` has no idle bound, by design ("pubsub and idle connection pools may keep a stream open indefinitely", `lego/operator/internal/kvinternalauth/proxy.go:166`).

## Done — 2026-11-20

- **Fix.** `sniproxy.CopyBidirectional` keeps one last-activity time per stream, shared by both directions (`streamBounds` in `lego/operator/internal/sniproxy/meter.go`). Each read's deadline is that time plus the idle bound, capped at the lifetime. A direction whose deadline passes while the other moved bytes reads on to the later deadline. So a stream closes only once neither direction has moved a byte for the bound, and at the 24 h lifetime however busy.
- **Tests** (`internal/sniproxy/limiter_test.go`, loopback TCP). `TestCopyBidirectionalOneWayTrafficKeepsTheStreamOpen` relays a write every 50 ms one way under a 300 ms bound for 900 ms, in each direction, then checks that the stream closes no sooner than the bound after its last bytes. `TestCopyBidirectionalMaxLifetimeEndsABusyStream` closes a busy stream at its lifetime. `TestCopyBidirectionalIdleTimeoutTearsDown` now also checks that a silent stream does not close before the bound. On the old `meter.go` the first test fails in both directions ("the stream closed 309ms after it opened … EOF"). Dropping the lifetime from the read-on check makes the second test fail.
- **Repro, local.** A throwaway test (not committed) ran a TLS `redis-server` 8.10.1 behind `kv-sni-proxy`'s `handleConn` with a 2 s idle bound. `redis-cli --tls SUBSCRIBE` went through the proxy while a publisher wrote every 0.5 s. On the old code the subscriber logged `Error: Server closed the connection` after 4 of 15 messages, at the bound, as on production. With the fix it received all 15 over 8.2 s.
- **Docs.** ADR009 § External connection admission gives the rule, and ADR021 (§ 9 External connections, § 10) and the operator guide's SNI rows say what idle means. The remaining gap (a stream silent both ways for 1 h, and every stream at 24 h) is recorded in ADR018 § Other datastores & storage as a divergence awaiting sign-off, owned by [w6/149](../blocked/149.md).
- **Rollout.** Both SNI binaries change. The next pin cannot come before 10:24Z (the 6 h rule after `a47204c7e`). That pin carries this commit together with `081c847e1`, and it moves both SNI pins anyway for `17e8848c0`, so this adds no roll. w6/m518 t006 reads the old pods' drain, which runs the binary this commit replaces.
