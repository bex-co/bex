# A disk service that probes by exec is down about 3 s longer on each deploy, because its health-probe install container starts at 20m CPU

Why: under `Recreate` every second before the new instance is Ready is downtime, and a disk-bearing service with a Health Check Path, or a web image service that names no port, pays about 3.3 s for bex's `bex-health-probe-install` init container on each deploy and restart. It is the throttled container start w6/m559 found and fixed for the disk holder (`5ada5fbcdc`). Render documents "a few seconds".

- **Found by:** w6/m547 t011, production pin `1a5a8e872398`, 2026-11-23, runs A1 and A2. They were code deploys of the disk-bearing Starter image service `w6-m547-t011` (disposable, `docker.io/traefik/whoami`, no `PORT`, no port) in `bex-canary`, on `bex-tenant-0-zdp6v-6p4cg`. The windows were 7.24 s (a cached tag) and 9.5 s (a new tag). With `PORT=80` set, so the TCP probe and no init container, eleven same-node runs of the same fixture took 3.99–4.26 s.
- **Sequence (A1, node syslog):** the new pod's sandbox returned at 07:13:27.972. containerd's `StartContainer` for the install container ran 27.993–30.039 (2.05 s; A2: 2.22 s). The container copied the binary and exited at 30.625 (0.59 s), and the app image's `PullImage` began at 31.332. The app container's own `StartContainer` took 0.17 s. From the pod reaching the node to the app running took 4.97 s, against 1.84–1.95 s for the named-port runs.
- **Where:** `healthProbeInstallContainer` (`lego/operator/internal/controller/health_probe.go:162`) sets requests and limits of 20m CPU and 32Mi. runc starts a container inside that container's cgroup, so at 1/50 of a CPU the start takes about 2 s, and the copy runs slowly too. w6/m559 t005 measured the same mechanism on the holder's pause pod: 25.4 s at 10m, 2.4 s at 100m and 1.4 s at 500m.
- **Who pays:** every pod that carries the helper (`usesHealthProbeBinary`, `deployment_projection.go:248`). For a disk service those are the ones with a Health Check Path (w8/m133) and a first web image release still discovering its port (w4/m350); a disk App has no drain check. Under `Recreate` it is downtime. A rolling service pays it too, but only as a slower rollout while the old instance serves.
- **Direction:** give the install container the app container's CPU, as requests and limits. The pod's effective request is the larger of an init container's and the sum of the app containers', so the pod's request, its QoS class and the plan's quota stay as they are. Test it the way `TestDiskHolderStartsWellWithinItsMountBound` tests the holder. Also check the 0.7 s between the install container's exit and the app's pull (the kubelet's next pass), which a faster start does not remove.
- **Done when:** on production, a disk-bearing Starter web image service in `bex-canary` with no `PORT`, or with a Health Check Path, has its app container running within about 2.2 s of its pod reaching the node, against 4.97 s in m547 t011. Each window is within 1 s of a named-port twin's, measured as in [m547 t011](m547/done/t011.md).

## 2026-11-23 — the fix shipped; the production measure waits for the pin

- **Fix (`12483e636f`).** The installer runs at the app container's CPU, as requests and limits, and keeps 32Mi (`healthProbeInstallResources`, `lego/operator/internal/controller/health_probe.go`). A pod's request is the larger of its largest init container's and its containers' sum, so the pod's request, its QoS class and the workspace quota do not move. An App with no tier names no CPU on either container, and the namespace's LimitRange gives both the same default. The installer's start and copy are about 53 ms of CPU (2.64 s at 1/50 of a CPU), so they should take about 0.1 s on Starter.
- **Adopted on the next roll.** A template whose installer runs at 20m keeps it until a deploy, a restart or a settings change rolls it, as the helper's image already was (`applyDeploymentSpec`). The operator upgrade that ships it rolls no service and Recreates no disk service. So the measure has to be a deploy or a restart, which it already is.
- **Tests.** `TestHealthProbeInstallerStartsAtTheAppContainersCPU` checks every compute tier: the installer's CPU equals the app container's, it never exceeds the containers' sum, its requests equal its limits, and on every paid tier the 53 ms takes at most 0.2 s. At 20m it fails (2.65 s). `TestHealthProbeInstallerCPUIsAdoptedOnTheNextRollNotOnUpgrade` fails when the lazy adoption is removed. `make test` and `make lint` passed.
- **The 0.7 s between the installer's exit and the app's pull** is the kubelet's generic PLEG, which relists the node's containers every second. The installer's exit is noticed on the next relist, and that pass starts the app. A faster installer does not shorten it, and no pod field does. Only a pod with no init container avoids it. A Kubernetes image volume (KEP-4639) could mount the helper from bex's image without one, but production runs Kubernetes v1.34.9 with only `UserNamespacesSupport` turned on, and `ImageVolume` is off by default there. That would be its own item. The wait falls anywhere in 0–1 s, so compare several runs with the twin's rather than one.
- **Expected on production.** About the named-port twin's 1.84–1.95 s, plus about 0.1 s for the installer and the 0–1 s relist: about 2.0–3.0 s from the pod reaching the node to the app running, against 4.97 s in t011.

## 2026-11-24 — measured on production: the installer no longer throttles

**Fixture.** `POST /v1/services` in `bex-canary` created the Starter image service `w6-170` (disposable; `srv-dc2l9q9o7jsc73b3phqg`, `docker.io/traefik/whoami:v1.10.1`) at 09:12:09Z. It had no port and no `PORT`, and the 1 GB disk `dsk-dc2l9q9o7jsc73b3phrg` at `/data`. It landed on `bex-tenant-0-zdp6v-swgkf`, whose kubelet reads `nodeStatusUpdateFrequency: 2s`. The new template's `bex-health-probe-install` asked for 500m CPU as requests and limits, the app container's CPU, and the pod stayed `Guaranteed`. The session was the QA user's (`scripts/qa-login.sh`), with a run-minted API key.

**Three configurations of the one fixture, so the twin shares the node.**

- **A, no `PORT`.** Code deploys carried the installer and the exec `open-port` startup probe. The restarts A3 and A5 carried no installer, because the release had found its port, so they count with the twin.
- **H, `PORT=80` and Health Check Path `/`.** Every pod carried the installer and the exec `check --port 80 --path /` probe. WH was the warm-up deploy that first carried it.
- **T, the named-port twin.** `PORT=80` and no Health Check Path: a TCP startup probe on port 80, and no init container. WT was its warm-up.

**Method.** As [m547 t011](m547/done/t011.md):

- a fresh-TLS `GET /` through `https://w6-170.onbex.co`, started every 250 ms;
- a locally stamped watch of the App's pods;
- the node's syslog (`/api/v1/nodes/<node>/proxy/logs/syslog`) for systemd, the kubelet and containerd.

The phases are:

- "installer start": containerd's `StartContainer` for the installer, to its return;
- "installer run": that return, to the installer's exit event;
- "exit → pull": that exit, to the app image's `PullImage`;
- "pull": the cached digest's registry check;
- "node → running": systemd creating the pod's cgroup, to the app's `StartContainer` returning.

A window runs from the first failing probe to the first 200 after it, read to the 0.25 s probe step.

**What the fix changed.**

- **The installer no longer throttles.** Its start took 0.10–0.15 s, against 2.05–2.22 s in t011, and it ran 0.01–0.03 s, against 0.59 s.
- **The app ran sooner.** With the installer, the app ran 1.67–2.64 s after its pod reached the node, 2.19 s on average, against 4.97 s in t011. The twin ran in 1.35–1.52 s.
- **The twin's other phases were unchanged.** The pod's sandbox came 0.58–0.67 s after its cgroup in every run, the old pod's teardown took 0.89–1.11 s, and the edge answered once the pod was Ready. All 21 instances ran on the volume's node. The volume kept its one `VolumeAttachment` from 09:12:16Z until the fixture was deleted, and the node logged no `DetachVolume` for it.

**What is left: the kubelet's relist, 0–1 s.**

- The wait from the installer's exit to the app's pull had two values: 0.08–0.17 s in five runs, and 0.97–1.04 s in six. That is the kubelet's PLEG relisting every second, as the 2026-11-23 record expected.
- **When the relist came at once,** the app ran in 1.67–1.80 s, and the windows were 4.25–4.75 s (A4, A6, H5, H6). That is within 0.75 s of the twin's 3.99–4.25 s.
- **When it waited the full second,** the app ran in 2.55–2.64 s, and the windows were 4.99–5.50 s (A1, WH, H1–H4). That is 1.0–1.5 s over the twin's.
- No pod field shortens the wait, so it is filed as its own item, [w6/176](../176.md).

**A2 had a separate excess.** A2's app ran 1.72 s after its pod reached the node, but the edge first answered 200 1.56 s after the pod turned Ready. The other no-port deploys answered within 0.12 s of Ready. The operator logged `Detected HTTP on port 80; serving it` at 09:15:04Z. This happened once in four no-port deploys. w6/162's production record measured 0.38–0.63 s for the same step, so A2 is not filed.

**Not counted.** Seven client timeouts were started at 09:16:53–09:16:55Z, between runs. No rollout ran then and no pod changed, so they are local network noise.

**Against the definition of done.**

- The cause this note names is gone. The installer's start and run fell from 2.64–2.81 s to 0.11–0.17 s.
- On average, the app runs within about 2.2 s of its pod reaching the node: 2.19 s, against 4.97 s.
- A window is within 1 s of the named-port twin's only when the kubelet's relist comes early. The full-second wait is the one term left, and the fix could not touch it, so [w6/176](../176.md) carries that bound.
