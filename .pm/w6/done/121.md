# A service deleted before its first hourly rollup still loses its egress

Why: since w6/m333 a deleted service's final hours of egress are metered from the router names its live egress meter recorded (`usage_egress_identities`). The meter records them in the hourly catch-up pass, so a service created and deleted between two rollups never has an identity, and its bandwidth is not billed. Render bills all outbound bandwidth ([outbound bandwidth](https://render.com/docs/outbound-bandwidth)). The gap is small, because such a service lived under an hour, but it is a real undercount.

- **Where:** `usage.catchUpAppMeter` records the identity; the operator creates the Ingress the routers come from (`lego/backend/internal/core/traefik.go` `TraefikRouterNames`).
- **Do:** record the identity when the App's Ingress first appears. One option is a cheap watch-driven pass in bex-api's usage service that resolves routers for Apps with no `usage_egress_identities` row; another is deriving the router names at deletion from the CR, while the Ingresses still exist. Test a service created at :21 and deleted at :27 gets its deletion-hour egress row. ~1h.
- **Source:** w6/m333 review (ADR023 § Deleted resources keep their final hours, Gap), 2026-11-12.

- **2026-11-12 (loopx w6):** done.
  - `apps.Service.Delete` records the egress identity before the row delete, while the CR and Ingresses still exist, so the retired pass has it even for an App no hourly rollup saw. It reaches the store through the optional `egressIdentityRecorder` interface (`*store.PGStore` is compile-checked) with a 5 s lookup bound, best-effort.
  - Test: `TestDeleteRecordsTheEgressIdentityWhileItsIngressExists`, mutation-checked. The retired pass that reads the identity is covered by w6/m333.
  - A workspace deletion cascade skips the verb and relies on the hourly record (ADR023).
