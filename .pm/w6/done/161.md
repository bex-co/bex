# A disk service's same-node Recreate can detach and reattach its volume, adding 25 s

Why: in one of ten same-node deploys and restarts of m547's fixture, the attach/detach controller detached the volume from the node the new instance was already bound to, and Hetzner's detach and attach added 25.6 s to a window that is otherwise 6–9 s. Render documents "a few seconds".

- **Found by:** w6/m547 t005, production, 2026-11-21, run D1 (code deploy `v1.10.2`, fixture `srv-dc0nm80n82tc73dukib0` in `bex-canary`, deleted since). The window was 31.75 s against 7.25–9.0 s for the other same-node code deploys.
- **Sequence (node `bex-tenant-0-zdp6v-6p4cg`, kubelet syslog and `kube-controller-manager` log):** the old pod turned `Failed` at 11:23:20.21. The kubelet unmounted the volume and marked it detached in its own state at 20.35. A node-status sync then reported it gone from `volumesInUse` before the pod object was deleted at 20.62. At 20.624 the controller logged `Verified volume is safe to detach`, because no scheduled pod used the volume at that instant: the new pod was bound to the same node 50 ms later. Detach took 9.4 s and attach 15.9 s, then the kubelet mounted as usual.
- **Why it is likelier now:** the controller detaches a volume that no scheduled pod uses once the node reports it free. The only cover between the old pod's teardown and the new pod's binding (0.3–0.6 s) is the delay before the node's next status sync. t010 (`e0b731eba`) cut tenant kubelets' `node-status-update-frequency` from 10 s to 2 s, which shortened the mount wait from 1.7–8.6 s to 0.03–1.96 s but makes a sync inside that gap about five times likelier. t001 saw no detach in its same-node runs at 10 s.
- **Options to settle:**
  - Keep the volume in use on its node through the swap: a minimal holder pod (`pause`, the same PVC, the same node, never writes) that the operator starts when a `Recreate` rollout begins and deletes once the new instance runs. It is skipped when the node is unschedulable, because the volume must then move. Check whether a mapping the holder keeps open also spares the new instance's LUKS open (about 2.2 s).
  - Or return to the 10 s sync, accepting the mount wait (a roll of the tenant pools).
- **Done when:** on production, at least ten consecutive same-node deploys and restarts of a disk service show no `DetachVolume` for its PV in the controller-manager log, measured as in m547 t005.

**Promoted 2026-11-21 to [w6/m559](../blocked/m559/README.md)** by `/loopx w6`. The premise holds on `90d1f6f6f`. No commit since the filing touches the disk rollout. `disk.go` sets `Recreate` and only a preference for the volume's node, and the four tenant templates still sync every 2 s. The first option is chosen: a holder Job's pod, on the volume's node, keeps the claim in use through the swap. The operator cannot author a bare pod or a `nodeName` (RBAC, `operator-workload-admission.yaml`), but it may author an App-owned Job. The 10 s sync is rejected: it lowers the race's odds from about 15–30 % to 3–6 % of swaps but does not close it, and it would bring back 1.7–8.6 s of mount wait on every same-node swap. With the holder, the 2 s sync stays. The milestone covers the Job, the rollout hook with its fallbacks, the readers that must not see the holder as an instance, the ADRs, a production measure of ten same-node runs, Simplify, Test coverage and Closeout.
