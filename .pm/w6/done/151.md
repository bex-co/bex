# A draining SNI pod's last stream, and every stream its bound closes, is never billed

Why: the SNI proxies count a stream's egress only when it closes, and a draining pod exits the moment its last stream ends. So on every SNI roll, the bytes of the longest-lived external Key Value and Postgres streams never reach Prometheus, and egress billing undercounts them.

- **Filed by** w6/m518 t006 (2026-11-20), from production pin `4e37ec03f` ([t006 § Step 2](done/m518/done/t006.md#step-2-the-sni-moving-deploy-2026-11-20-pin-4e37ec03f)). Each counter below is `bex_{kv,pg}_proxy_egress_bytes_total` for a probe store's series on one old pod.
  - `bex-kv-sni-proxy-vbv9g` read 47,413,721 until its last scrape at 08:53:21.8Z. Its last stream, a 20-min session that had read about 15.7 MB, closed at 08:53:33.9Z, and the pod was gone before the next scrape.
  - `bex-pg-sni-proxy-8js68` read 38,456,330 until 08:56:20.2Z. Its last stream, a session of the same size, closed at 08:56:27.1Z.
  - On `-z28jk`, `-5wkfv` and `-w9kl8`, the bound closed held clients connected since 06:16Z–06:33Z. Each counter was flat from 08:52Z to the pod's last scrape, seconds before the close.
- **Mechanism.**
  - `sniproxy.CopyBidirectional` (`lego/operator/internal/sniproxy/meter.go`) calls `meter.Add` only once the backend→client copy ends.
  - `sniproxy.Serve` (`serve.go`) returns as soon as a drain's last handler returns.
  - `main` in `cmd/kv-sni-proxy` and `cmd/pg-sni-proxy` returns right after `Serve`, and its deferred `metricsServer.Close()` closes the listener. So the last increment is never scraped by the `bex-kv-proxy` and `bex-pg-proxy` jobs (`deploy/gitops/base/prometheus.yaml`).
  - The same holds for every open stream when a pod crashes or its node is lost.
  - w6/146 and w6/m515 recorded that a stream closing within one scrape of its process's exit is never billed. Production shows that this covers every draining pod's last stream and every stream the bound closes. Before w6/m515, a roll lost every open stream's bytes.
- **Directions to weigh.**
  - Count bytes as they are copied, for example by adding each write or flushing every few seconds. A long stream is then billed as it runs, in the hours it ran, and a crash loses at most the unflushed part.
  - Or keep the metrics listener up for a scrape interval plus a margin after `Serve` returns. That covers only the clean exit, and the grace period must grow to cover it: 3,610 s is the 5 s pause, the 3,600 s bound and 5 s of margin (`TestTheDaemonSetGivesTheDrainItsBound`).
  - Keep ADR023 § The WebSocket and datastore sources holding (a series appears at its first bytes and is never deleted), with `TestByteMeterSeriesAppearsAtFirstBytesAndStays`.
- **Done when:**
  - On the local cluster, a draining pod's last stream and a stream its bound closes both show their bytes in a scrape of that pod before it exits.
  - A regression test in `internal/sniproxy` fails on today's close-time count.
  - ADR021 § 10 and ADR023 state the rule.

## Outcome (2026-11-20, w6 `/loopx` worker)

**Done.** Both directions the note weighed, together:

- **Counted as delivered.** `sniproxy.CopyBidirectional` hands each backend→client write to the meter as it lands, through a read-write loop for every stream (`io.Copy` splices on Linux and reports nothing until the stream ends). `TestAnOpenStreamsBytesAreCountedAsTheyReachTheClient` reads an open stream's bytes from the counter over loopback TCP; on the close-time count it read 0.
- **The exit follows a scrape.** After a drain, both mains wait up to `sniproxy.FinalScrapeWait` (35 s) for a scrape that began after the last stream ended, told from the readiness probe by Prometheus's `X-Prometheus-Scrape-Timeout-Seconds` header (`ByteMeter.Handler`, `AwaitFinalScrape`, `TestAwaitFinalScrapeReturnsJustAfterAScrapeReadTheLastBytes`). A pod with nothing new waits too, because an exit just before a scrape left `up` 0 on production at 09:51:11Z (`bex-kv-sni-proxy-5wkfv`) and failed the hour ending 10:00Z for every Key Value store. Both DaemonSets' grace period is 3,645 s (5 + 3,600 + 35 + 5), held by `TestTheDaemonSetGivesTheDrainItsBound` and `scripts/gitops-validate.sh`.
- **Local cluster**, final build `bex-operator:w6-151b`, `pg-sni-proxy` with a local-only 120 s bound, a sampler scraping every 15 s with Prometheus's header, and an external TLS `psql` session reading 256 KiB every 5 s through the public probe `dpg-w6151probe` in dev-6's tenant namespace:
  - The bound closed a held session at 11:27:41Z; the scrape at 11:27:55Z read 8,933,581 bytes, exactly the client socket's `bytes_received` less the proxy's own `S`, where the scrape before the close read 8,670,911. The process logged `drained scraped=true` 0.17 s later and exited.
  - A session that was its pod's last stream quit 26 s into the drain (11:29:15Z); the scrape the process waited for, at 11:29:26Z, read 3,154,869 (the socket's count less the `S`, plus the server's 24-byte `close_notify`), and the pod exited.
  - An idle `kv-sni-proxy` pod exited just after the first scrape after its `SIGTERM`.
  - The probe, its NodePort and the copied credentials are deleted. Both DaemonSets run `bex-operator:w6-151b` with the repository's drain and grace.
- **Docs.** ADR021 § 10, ADR023 § The WebSocket and datastore sources, ADR009 and `lego/operator/AGENTS.md` state the rule. The bill still skips the hours a pod starts or exits early in, for every store: filed as [w6/155](../155.md).
- **Production.** The ship rolls both DaemonSets, twice if Argo applies the new grace period at push on the running image before the image pin moves both SNI pins. The old pods of those rolls run the old binary, so the first roll whose old pods count as they copy is the next one that moves an SNI pin after this ship. It moves both SNI pins, so its pin range cannot be w6/150's CRD-only `kept` check, which waits for a later pin.
