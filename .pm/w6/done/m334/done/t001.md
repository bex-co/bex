## Objective

bex-api publishes `bex_usage_oldest_unmetered_hour_age_seconds{source}`: across every open (or this month's closed) coverage stream, how old the oldest hour inside the catch-up window is that has no health row or an `unavailable` one.

## Context

- `store.UsageCatchupWindow` (48 h) and `usageSettledBefore` (49 h with the disk meter's extra hour): an hour older than that is skipped for good (w4/496)
- `usage_source_streams`, `usage_source_health`; the rollup loop in `usage.Service.RunWithIntervals`
- No existing alert watches the rollup's lag: the EgressMeter* alerts watch the meters, the Billing* alerts the export

## Steps

1. Add one fleet-wide store read (per source: the oldest missing or unavailable expected hour inside the window).
2. Publish it after each rollup.
3. Unit-test the read on a store with a hole.

- lego/backend/internal/store/usage.go

## Done 2026-11-12

- `store.OldestUnmeteredUsageHours` is one fleet-wide read over `[usageSettledBefore, latestClosed)`. It shares the coverage query (`usageCoverageStreams`) and the watermark walk (`evaluateStreamCoverage`), and reports per source the oldest hour with no healthy or degraded reading.
- bex-api publishes it after every pass and once before the startup catch-up, as `bex_usage_oldest_unmetered_hour_timestamp_seconds{source}` (`internal/usage/metrics.go`). The value is the hole's start, 0 with none, and the read has a 30 s timeout.
- **Changed from the plan:** the plan named an age gauge, `…_age_seconds`. The /simplify review found that an age freezes when publishing stops, so a 20 h hole would never reach 24 h. A start time ages from `time()` in the alert instead, and the panel shows the age.
- Tests: `TestOldestUnmeteredUsageHoursPG`, which covers a missing or unavailable hour, a degraded hour counting as metered, a stream ended earlier, and an hour past the window. `TestPublishUnmeteredHoursSetsEverySourcesStart` covers the reset to 0.
