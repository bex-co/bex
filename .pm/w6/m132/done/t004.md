## Objective

The acceptance script for this surface already exists and is thorough. It simply never runs, which is why a total loss of the SSH handshake went unnoticed for weeks. Close that gap — and close it once, because this is the second instance of the same institutional problem.

## Context

- `scripts/ssh-verify.sh` (17,976 bytes) calls itself "Redacting public-edge acceptance for `docs/ADR035-ssh.md`". `ADR035:157` documents its matrix. It is not wired to any schedule.
- **`w6/m131/t004` owes the identical decision for `scripts/logs-verify.sh`** — that milestone exists because the request-log pipeline silently produced nothing in production, with its own written-but-unrun acceptance script. Two features, two acceptance scripts, neither running. Prefer one mechanism over two.
- A minimal always-on synthetic is unusually cheap here: the version-exchange + "did KEXINIT arrive" check needs **no credentials, no fixture, and no service** — it is the raw-socket probe from the milestone README. The full `ssh-verify.sh` matrix needs a paid fixture and is better suited to a scheduled run.
- That split is worth stating explicitly: a cheap continuous liveness check plus a periodic deep matrix, rather than choosing one.

## Steps

1. Decide the mechanism, in coordination with `w6/m131/t004`.
2. Implement the cheap KEXINIT-liveness synthetic.
3. Wire `scripts/ssh-verify.sh` into whatever scheduled gate is chosen.
4. Demonstrate the guard fails against today's broken state and passes after `t001`'s fix.
