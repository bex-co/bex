## Objective

Ship after w6/m470 t006's held-client deploy. Then confirm on production that the shipping deploy rolled each SNI DaemonSet once by surge with no failed new external connection, and that across the first later deploy that moves an SNI pin, a held external client mid-command gets its reply through each proxy.

## Context

- The milestone's sequencing constraint: do not ship until m470 t006 has recorded its kept-pin deploy, or m470 is in `done/`.
- The shipping deploy moves both SNI pins (`scripts/operator-pins.sh`). Its roll is a surge, because the new pods ask for no host port, but it still closes the old pods' streams at once, because they run the old code.
- Up to about 40% of production pins change an SNI binary (22 of 56, an upper bound, ADR021 § 10), so a later moving deploy is likely within a day or two. `git log origin/main --grep "pin platform images"` lists the pins, and each write-back logs `kept` or `moved` per proxy.
- Production access: `bash scripts/fetch-app-kubeconfig.sh <path outside the repo>`. Probes go in `bex-canary`. External paths go through the edge LB: `<id>.kv.bex.co:6379`, `<id>.db.bex.co:5432`. Each probe's allowlist must admit the client. Streams close after 1 h idle and 24 h in total, so a held client keeps sending and starts less than 24 h before the deploy it watches.
- Production runs Cilium. Whether it keeps an established NodePort flow to a terminating pod is first proven by the held client here.

## Steps

1. Ship. On the first production pin whose producer contains the commit, record that each DaemonSet rolled once: two pods per node briefly, no `hostPort`, the drain env and the grace period. A loop opening new external connections during that deploy logs no failure.
2. Hold a Key Value client in a `BLPOP` and a Postgres client in a long `pg_sleep` (or a client sending a request every few seconds), plus an idle client on each. Leave them across the next deploy that moves an SNI pin. Record the replies, the idle clients' close times against the old pods' `SIGTERM`, and the new-connection loop.
3. Delete the probes.
