## Objective

Work the list the censuses produce: each flagged table gets a real answer — a cascade, a purger entry, or a written retention rationale — with no entry left as "unknown".

## Context

- Expected list from the research (verify against the live census before acting; do not trust this list blindly):
  - `jobs` (migration 0027, `tenant_id`, no FK, no purger, no retention sweep) — one-off job records that outlive their workspace forever. A cascade is almost certainly right; confirm nothing reads `jobs` by id without a tenant guard first (`store/jobs.go:72,95,148` all scope by `tenant_id`).
  - `agent_session_dispatches` (0105) — deliberately **not** FK'd to `agent_sessions`: the recovery worker carries a `SessionDeleted` flag and still tears down a previous sandbox after the session row is gone (`store/agentsessions.go:547`, `store/agentdispatch.go:58`). This is retention-with-a-reason, but the reason is currently only implicit in a query. Declare it, and confirm the abandoned-tombstone cleanup actually bounds the table.
  - `datastore_event_facts`, `datastore_observed_checkpoints`, `service_event_index` — event projections; check each for an existing retention sweep before adding a cascade.
  - `workspace_creation_attempts` — has a 30-day terminal-row sweep (`store/workspace_creation.go:258-265`); that is retention, and t004 handles its identity columns.
- Prefer a cascade over a purger where the data is pure workspace state: a cascade cannot be forgotten at the composition root.

## Steps

1. Run both censuses; take their actual output as the work list.
2. For each entry decide cascade / purger / retention, with the reason recorded in the census inventory.
3. Write migrations for the cascades (up and down, verified against real Postgres).
4. Re-run both censuses to green.
