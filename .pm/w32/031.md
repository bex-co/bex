# A setting saved while a deploy waits on a throttled or failing image registry does not reach the serving release until the registry answers

Why: on Render, IP rules and suspension act on a service at once, even while a deploy is in progress. A served image service whose newest deploy waits out a Docker Hub rate limit (429), a 5xx or a network error ignores an IP allow-list, a suspend or a workspace rule for as long as the wait lasts.

- **Found by:** w32/m419's Render parity task, 2026-11-16, from the code; not reproduced live.
- **Where:** `Reconcile` in `lego/operator/internal/controller/app_controller.go`, the image-port branch. `imageRegistryWait` (`image_network.go`) returns the wait's requeue before `failBeforeDispatch` or `dispatchRuntime` runs. The registry read gate's `registryBusyError` returns the same way. Each retry backs off from 30 s to 5 minutes and repeats until the registry answers, and Docker Hub's anonymous limit can last hours.
- **Shape of the fix:** over a served release, hold the serving release while the read waits, as w32/m419 does for a refused read: call `holdPendingArtifact` with the wait's requeue as its `buildHalt`, and keep the Deploying phase and the wait's stall message (`ImageRegistryRateLimited` / `ImageRegistryUnavailable`). Do not record a Rollout verdict: the deploy is still open and must still go live once the registry answers.
- **Done when:** an envtest App with a served release, whose newest deploy's registry read answers 429, projects a changed IP allow-list Middleware and parks on suspend while the deploy stays Deploying with its rate-limit stall message. Once the registry answers 200, the deploy goes live.
- **Estimate:** ~1h. Minor: the wait ends by itself, unlike w32/m419's refusal.
