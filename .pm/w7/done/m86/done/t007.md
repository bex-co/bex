## Outcome

`/simplify` ran as three parallel reviews (reuse, quality, efficiency). Applied: a shared `skopeoCopyArgs` that derives the TLS-verify flag from which SIDE is the registry (the arg assembly existed three times, and the src/dest asymmetry was a standing hazard); a shared `pushPhaseResources`; phase/volume name constants beside the existing `pushContainer`; and reuse of the package's existing `containerByName`/`mountByName`/`volByName`/`contNames` test helpers.

Three findings were real defects rather than cleanups, and are the reason this task earned its keep:

1. **The pod's ephemeral-storage ceiling had silently doubled, 16G → 32G.** A pod's limit is the SUM of its regular containers', so giving `cache-save` the push phase's 16G undid the bound `build.go` was written to enforce. Fixed with a dedicated 8G cache bound (ceiling now 24G) and 8G cache emptyDirs, and pinned by a test that adds the limits up the way kubelet does — no existing test could see it, because they all looked at one container at a time.
2. **Neither skopeo cache call had a timeout.** The best-effort wrapper catches a non-zero exit, not a hang: a stuck registry connection would have consumed the Job's whole 30-minute deadline and then failed a build whose image was already pushed — exactly what the cache invariant forbids. Both phases now carry `--command-timeout 5m`.
3. **A comment of mine was false.** It claimed running `cache-save` beside the push keeps the cache off the deploy's critical path; the Job is Complete only when every regular container exits, so the deploy waits for `max(push, cache-save)`. Parallel is still the right call (1.5s vs 2.3s serialized) but the comment now states what actually happens.

Also fixed, from the reuse review: **the cache repository was never deleted when its App was**, orphaning the larger of the two artifacts in a repository whose ACL had just been revoked — unreachable as well as unowned. `deleteRegistryRepo` now reclaims it, and a tombstoned App drops its legacy cache grant too.

Gate-off byte-identity was re-verified against HEAD after the refactor (the push container is on that path), and both sample shapes still hash identically.
