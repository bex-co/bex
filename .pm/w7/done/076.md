# A Pro workspace's sandbox quota stops at 116 sandboxes, below the 1,000 its plan allows

Why: since w7/069 a Pro or Scale workspace may have 1,000 concurrent sandboxes, as on Render, but its sandbox namespace's quota refuses the 117th default-sized one, after a 330 s wait and with the wrong answer.

- Found by w7/069, 2026-11-17.
- Render: "Render Pro defaults to 1,000 per workspace, with up to 5,000 on request" (render.com/articles/render-sandboxes-vs-vercel-sandboxes, 2026-11-06); render.com/pricing's "Default concurrent sandboxes" row reads 100 | 1,000 | 1,000 | Custom.
- bex: the admission cap is `core.ConcurrentSandboxes` (100 on Hobby, 1,000 on Pro and higher; `lego/backend/internal/sandbox/admission.go`, `workspaceLimit`). But `<ws>-sandbox` carries the workspace's plan quota (`store.quotaForPlan`, `lego/backend/internal/store/namespaces.go`): 116 CPU and 264Gi of limits, so at most 116 sandboxes of the default `1c-2g` (1 CPU, 2Gi), and 15,121Gi of ephemeral-storage limits, which hold 378 sandboxes at 40Gi. The 117th `1c-2g` sandbox is refused at pod admission; OpenSandbox waits for a pod that never comes, and bex-api answers `409 SANDBOX_CAPACITY_LIMIT` ("upgrade the plan") after the pod-ready wait, on a plan with nothing to upgrade to. Hobby's 100 fits inside both.
- The same was already true of a group's 1,000 (ADR104 § Numbers are targets, not capacity), so this is not new with w7/069, but the plan cap makes it reachable sooner.
- ADR042 § Sandbox groups ("Capacity binds first on Pro and higher") and the ADR018 row "Concurrent sandboxes per workspace" record it.
- Done when: a Pro workspace can hold its plan's concurrent sandboxes of the default plan, or a create past what its quota holds is refused at once with a coded 429 that names the limit instead of after the wait. Pick the option that matches Render (the first), unless sizing the sandbox quota to 1,000 sandboxes is a capacity decision only the user can make, in which case ship the fast refusal and record the gap in ADR018. Pin it with a test against `quotaForPlan` for the sandbox regime.

**Done 2026-11-17 (w7/076): Render's option, the quota holds the plan's number.** The quota was tighter than this note says: a `1c-2g` pod charges 4Gi of memory limits, because its `execd-installer` init container takes the sandbox LimitRange's 4Gi default (ADR104 D8, read on production for w22/020). So 264Gi held 66 default sandboxes on every plan, Hobby's 100 included. Sizing the quota is not the capacity decision. The quota provisions nothing, and the sandbox pool is one fixed `cpx42` (`sandbox-pool.yaml`: `replicas: 1`, no autoscaler annotations) whose size ADR105 leaves to the user, so the fast-refusal fallback was not needed. `<ws>-sandbox` now gets its plan's quota plus room for `core.ConcurrentSandboxes(plan)` pods of the largest sandbox plan (`store.quotaForRegime`, `sandboxQuotaRoom`, `SandboxPodQuotaMax`). The quota therefore never refuses before the 429, whatever the plans; a Pro sandbox namespace allows 8,116 CPU and 1,500 pods. Hosting namespaces are unchanged, and the reconciler converges existing namespaces at its next pass. `TestSandboxQuotaHoldsThePlansConcurrentSandboxes` and `TestReconcileGivesTheSandboxNamespaceItsRoom` fail with the room removed. `TestEverySandboxPlanFitsTheQuotaRoomPerSandbox` and `TestSandboxPodQuotaMaxCoversTheInitContainer` pin the per-pod figure from both sides. ADR042 § Sandbox groups, ADR104 (Plans, Numbers are targets) and the ADR018 row record it. What still binds is the pool: it fits fewer than 30 `1c-2g` sandboxes across every workspace, and a create it cannot place answers `503 SANDBOX_START_FAILED` after the pod-ready wait.
