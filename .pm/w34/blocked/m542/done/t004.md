## Objective

m541's ops verb also takes a subscriber request (database id, subscriber role; on 13–15 the publisher connection and publication name if t001 chose the CNPG `Subscription`) and checks the same prerequisites: a Pro (or higher) workspace and at least 10 GB of storage.

## Context

- m541/t006's verb, refusals and audit event (`lego/backend/internal/postgres/logical_replication.go`).
- Render's form covers "both publishers _and_ subscribers", so one database may be both.

## Steps

1. Accept the subscriber form; reuse m541's refusals; add `LOGICAL_REPLICATION_ROLE_UNKNOWN` for the subscriber role.
2. Record enable and disable in the audit log; extend `docs/runbooks/postgres-logical-replication.md`.

## Done (2026-11-21)

- The verb takes `--subscriber-role` (16 and later) and `--subscription <name> --publisher-url - --publication <name>` (13–15; the URL is read from stdin only, so its password is on no command line and in no `exec` audit record), with m541's prerequisites and refusals. `LOGICAL_REPLICATION_ROLE_UNKNOWN` covers the subscriber role; `LOGICAL_REPLICATION_SUBSCRIBER_FORM` refuses the form that does not fit the major; malformed subscription requests are `LOGICAL_REPLICATION_INVALID_REQUEST`, never repeating the URL.
- An enable sets the halves it names and keeps the others; `disable` takes `--publisher`, `--subscriber` or `--subscription <name>`. Enable and disable record the same audit rows as the publisher's. The 13–15 password lives in a Secret per subscription, owned by the Database; a new password gets a new Secret so CNPG rewrites the passfile, and the replaced or removed Secret is deleted.
- The runbook has "Enable a subscriber" and the subscriber half of "Disable", including the recovery for a subscription whose drop can no longer authenticate.
- Every refusal writes nothing: `TestLogicalReplicationSubscriberRefusesBeforeWriting` (no spec change, no Secret, no audit row) and the real-PostgreSQL test.
