# A logical-replication subscriber with a high-availability standby can skip its last transactions at a failover

Why: bex's HA standbys are asynchronous, so after a failover a bex subscriber can silently miss rows its publisher already counts as delivered; Render documents HA failover for every paid Postgres and bex marks it ✅ (found by w34/m543, 2026-11-21).

- **Premise.** A subscription and its replication origin are WAL-logged, so they reach the standby, but asynchronously. The apply worker reports a transaction to the publisher as flushed once its local commit is flushed on the primary. A standby promoted before receiving those commits resumes from its own, older origin position, while the publisher's slot has already confirmed past it, so the walsender starts from the slot's position and the transactions in between are never applied. ADR009 § Lifecycle › Failover and switchover and the ADR018 row "Logical replication (lifecycle)" record the gap.
- **Paths:** `lego/operator/internal/controller/database_controller.go` (`cnpgClusterSpec`: HA sets no synchronous replication), `lego/operator/internal/controller/database_subscription.go`, `docs/ADR009-postgresql-management.md`.
- **Options to weigh:** a synchronous standby (CNPG `postgresql.synchronous` with `dataDurability: required`) for a database enabled as a subscriber, which costs write latency and availability while the standby is down; or documenting a re-sync after a failover, as for HA 13–16 publishers. Check first what Render does ([w34/001](blocked/001.md)).
- **End state:** on an HA PostgreSQL 18 subscriber taking a counted stream from an outside publisher, a forced failover loses and duplicates no row, or the gap is a signed-off divergence in ADR018 and the verb's answer warns about it.

**Closed 2026-11-21 by `/loopx w34`: Render's documented HA, warned about, not a divergence.** Reproduced locally on PostgreSQL 18.7 with a publisher, a subscriber and its streaming standby: after a failover the new primary had 2,000 of 3,000 rows, the 1,000 applied last skipped and none duplicated, and a switchover lost nothing. Render's standby is asynchronous too, and Render documents that "changes to your primary instance in the last few seconds before the failover will be lost" ([render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability), read 2026-11-21). A subscriber's applied changes are such changes, so bex matches Render and no sign-off is needed; the end state's "signed-off divergence" branch assumed a divergence that does not exist. The verb's answer now warns an HA subscriber on every major (`failoverWarnings`; `TestFailoverWarnings`, and `TestHASubscriberEnableWarnsAboutFailover` on real PostgreSQL), and [ADR009 § Lifecycle](../../../docs/ADR009-postgresql-management.md#lifecycle-w34m543), the ADR018 "Logical replication (lifecycle)" row, the runbook and the `bex-postgres` agent skill say so. The first option alone would not have closed the gap: a synchronous standby lost the same 1,000 rows while the subscription kept PostgreSQL's default `synchronous_commit = off`. That stricter-than-Render variant is [w34/005](../005.md).
