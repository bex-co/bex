# 008 — An old primary that rejoins after a clean failover keeps stale logical slots, and slot synchronization stops on it

Why: on an HA publisher on PostgreSQL 17 or later, the next failover or switchover onto that instance no longer carries a `failover = true` subscription's slot as synchronized. It reuses a stale copy, which holds back WAL on the standby until `max_slot_wal_keep_size` invalidates it, and the subscription then has to be re-created.

## Evidence (production pin `7e481dece7ac`, 2026-11-21, found by w34/m543's replay)

HA PostgreSQL 18.6 publisher `dpg-dc0s50i5r8pc73e308p0`, slot synchronization on (w34/m543 t001), an outside PostgreSQL 18 subscriber with `s_ha` (`failover = true`) and `s_nofo` (without):

- Before: the standby listed `s_ha` with `synced = t`; `s_nofo` was not on it.
- `kubectl delete pod <primary>` (smart shutdown, about three minutes while a writer stayed connected) promoted the standby. The old primary rejoined without `pg_rewind` and kept both slots with `synced = f`, at the position it stopped. Its log repeated `exiting from slot synchronization because same name slot "s_ha" already exists on the standby` for as long as it was the standby.
- A switchover back to it (`POST /v1/postgres/{id}/failover`) streamed `s_ha` from that stale copy (`logical decoding found consistent point at 0/708D198`). Nothing was lost or duplicated in this run, only because the copy still held all the WAL. `s_nofo`, which had read `replication slot "s_nofo" does not exist` on the other primary, resumed as well.
- After a forced Pod deletion (`--grace-period=0 --force`), `pg_rewind` ran on the old primary, its slots were gone, and it logged `newly created replication slot "s_ha" is sync-ready now`. So only a clean rejoin keeps the stale slots.

## Direction

- PostgreSQL's slot sync worker never replaces a slot it did not create (`synced = f`). The rejoined standby's logical slots with `synced = false` have to be dropped once it runs as a standby of an HA publisher with `synchronizeLogicalDecoding`: by CloudNativePG (check releases after 1.30.1 and its issue tracker) or by bex's operator through a superuser connection to the replica.
- Record the outcome in ADR009 § Lifecycle › Failover and switchover.

## Done when

- After a Pod-deletion failover on an HA 17+ publisher, the rejoined standby lists every failover-enabled slot with `synced = t` within the sync interval and holds no logical slot with `synced = f`.
- A second failover or switchover back loses and duplicates no row on a `failover = true` subscriber, on a real cluster.
