# Snapshot GC can reclaim the served release's record and config snapshots while a newer release has not served

Why: `gcReleaseConfigSnapshots` (`lego/operator/internal/controller/release_config_snapshot.go`) counts its 20-generation window back from `status.releaseGeneration`, the release being rolled, not from the release that serves. When the served release is 20 or more generations behind the one that just applied (operational edits such as scale and suspend move `metadata.generation` without a deploy), its `<app>-podtemplate-r<N>` record and its configuration snapshot Secrets are deleted as soon as the new template lands. If that rollout then fails or is canceled, w1/m152 and w1/m172 go back to the served release: the record is gone (m172 falls back to the retained ReplicaSet, a cancel re-projects), and a restored template can name snapshot Secrets that no longer exist, so its pods cannot start.

**Source:** `/simplify` review of w1/m172, 2026-10-03. Read from the code, not reproduced.

- **Check first:** whether a served release's snapshots are labelled with its own generation (`snapshotGenerationLabel`) or with an earlier `configSnapshotGeneration` they were reused from; the exemption must cover whatever the served template references.
- **Fix direction:** never reclaim at or above `successfulReleaseGeneration(app)` while it differs from `status.releaseGeneration`. A first attempt (`cutoff = served - 1`) broke `TestGCReleaseConfigSnapshotsKeepsWindowAndOwnership` and `TestAdoptUnscopedSnapshotsKeepsTheTemplateAndCoOwns`, whose fixtures set `releaseGeneration` far ahead of the served revision, so the co-owned unscoped copies (`adoptUnscopedSnapshots`) need the same thought.
- **Done when** an envtest or fake-client test shows a served release 20+ generations behind a failed rollout keeps its record and snapshots, and they are reclaimed once a newer release serves.
