## Objective

Establish what the shared harness must do, by diffing the ten copies and deciding — per divergence — whether it is a real capability to keep or accidental drift to drop.

## Context

- Normalized (`dev-N`/`wN`/`N` substituted) hashes at HEAD:
  - `up.sh` — **8 distinct variants across 10 copies**. Only `w2`/`w7`/`w10` agree (175 lines). Sizes: `w10`/`w2`/`w7` 175, `w6` 177, `w4` 181, `w1` 183, `w8` 188, `w9` 189, `w3` 199, `w5` **274**.
  - `status.sh` — 5 variants. `w1`/`w2`/`w4`/`w7`/`w8`/`w10` agree (41 lines); `w3` and `w9` differ at 42; `w6` differs at 41; `w5` is 109.
  - `down.sh` — fully converged, all ten identical (33 lines). This is the proof convergence is achievable.
- Known real divergence: `w1/dev-1/up.sh` forwards a Mailpit SMTP port and exports `BEX_SMTP_ADDR`, `BEX_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_INVITE_EMAIL`, `BEX_CP_INSECURE`; `w2/dev-2` has none of it.
- The static YAML (`db/*.yaml`, `mailpit/`, `values/`, `rbac-dev-N.yaml`) is identical modulo the N substitution — those are templates, not divergences.
- `w5`'s 274-line `up.sh` is the outlier; assume it has the most accumulated capability and read it first.

## Steps

1. Normalize and diff all ten `up.sh` and all ten `status.sh` pairwise; produce the union of behaviours.
2. Classify each divergence: keep (real capability), drop (dead or superseded), or per-workstream override.
3. Pay particular attention to environment variables exported to the local `bex-api` — that is where the drift changes what is testable.
4. Decide the override mechanism for anything genuinely per-workstream, beyond the port derivation `ports.env` already handles.
5. Record the union, the drops with reasons, and the override list.
