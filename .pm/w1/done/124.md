## Resolution 2026-10-25: cause named, fix ready, no production access needed

**Cause: the third candidate, measured.** Traffic does not matter. A Deployment that completed at zero replicas keeps Progressing at `True/NewReplicaSetAvailable` when it is scaled up, so Kubernetes never times out its unready pods.

- **Live test on the kind management cluster** (Kubernetes 1.36.1, `progressDeadlineSeconds: 20`). The test parked a Deployment at 0, wrote a never-ready template onto it, then scaled it to 1. After 75s it still read `Progressing=True/NewReplicaSetAvailable` with `Available=False`. A control Deployment that rolled the same template while awake reached `ProgressDeadlineExceeded` at about 30s.
- **How the operator reacts.** `releaseFacts.awaitingVerdict` therefore stays true forever (`newer`, no verdict, `templateCurrent`, scaled, no deadline). `planRelease` never parks the App, and `reportRolloutProgress` never settles it. The service reads Deploying behind the activator whatever its traffic is.
- **Why the other two candidates are not it.** The idle and traffic check is never consulted while a verdict is awaited.
- **The forensic window closed.** Prometheus keeps 3 days and Loki 21 days, so 2026-10-04's series and logs expired around 2026-10-25 13:40Z. They are not needed: the mechanism is reproduced.

**Fix (working tree, awaiting `/ship`).** The new `rolloutDeadline(app, dep)` gives a release the verdict its rollout would have had. It applies only to a release that has not served, on a scaled Deployment that Kubernetes reports as `NewReplicaSetAvailable` while unavailable for longer than `rolloutBudgetSeconds` (Render's 15 minutes, counted from `Available=False`). `facts()`, the Deploying stamp and `reportRolloutProgress` all read it. A newer release then settles failed, and `actHoldVerdict` restores the served template. A first release stops Failed (w1/m179). A served release whose pods go unready gets no deploy verdict.

- Tests in `scaled_up_release_test.go` cover the newer release, the idle park, a resumed first release, and the served-release guard. The first three failed before the fix.
- `make test` and `make lint` pass.

**One-time production check: superseded by the fix.** Every App left in this state is reconciled every 5s, and its unavailability is far past the budget. After deploy, its next pass settles it and puts the served release back. No suspend and resume is needed, and no production read is needed to find it.

**Remaining:** `/ship`, then the deploy. After that, a QA spot-check that no service in the QA-visible workspaces reads Deploying for longer than 15 minutes.
