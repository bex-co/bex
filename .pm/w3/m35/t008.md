## Objective

Prove the hardened boundary on the real containerd/Cilium/gVisor substrate with attacker-controlled code, not only rendered YAML or unit tests.

## Context

- m32 validated create/list/stop and gVisor kernel identity but did not test two same-workspace sandboxes against each other or DNS tunneling.
- The existing tenant isolation matrix covers hosting namespaces, not the sandbox-specific direct-Pod, execd, DNS, and lifecycle-server paths.

## Steps

1. Create two sandboxes in workspace A and one in workspace B; probe Pod IP, Service, execd, egress API, user ports, hosting namespace, platform services, API server, nodes, metadata, DNS exfiltration, no/wrong-SNI IP traffic, private/rebound targets, approved FQDN+SNI, and lifecycle API. Prove both lifecycle and execd ingress reject workload-label spoofing without the exact ServiceAccount.
2. Exercise owner/member/admin control-plane operations across both workspace and owner boundaries.
3. Verify server/controller-SA permissions; prove controller-submitted runc and create-time `nodeName` Pods are denied while the exact gVisor shape is admitted; exercise the NamespaceReconciler admission guard with impersonated malicious and canonical dry-run requests; capture Cilium/Hubble deny evidence and clean up all fixtures.
