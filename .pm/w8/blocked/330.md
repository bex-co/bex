# A slow request that wakes a free web service is cut with the activator's 502 12–33 seconds after the wake, in 4 of 5 runs; Render holds it through the spin-up and serves it

Why: w8/178 made bex do what Render does for the first request to a spun-down free service: hold it through the spin-up and serve it. For a quick answer that works (sweep `77c3`: a plain client got 200 after 11.6 s).

For a slow one it mostly does not. In 4 of 5 runs a request held through the wake was cut while the woken app was still working on it:

- the client got `502 {"error":"bad gateway"}` with `x-render-routing: no-server`;
- the cut came 12–33 s after `service_woken`;
- one run in five was served.

A webhook, bot, export or AI call that takes more than a few seconds on its cold-start request fails where Render serves it.

**Severity:** major (a request Render serves is dropped mid-exchange, on the cold path every free service hits). **Attribution:** platform edge or runtime on the wake path. The cause is **unverified**: production operator and activator logs are needed (the `w8/287` gate). **Estimate:** ~1–2h once logs are available. **Source:** `/qa-find-bugs-cli` w8 loop sweep `1298`, 2026-11-14 13:36–14:31 UTC, production after the 13:10 UTC roll of `c99e7815e`, workspace `bex-canary`, released `bex v0.6.0`.

## Evidence

**Fixtures:**

- `qa-20261114-1298-wake`: `bex services create --type web_service --image docker.io/traefik/whoami:v1.10.3 --plan free`; whoami listens on 80, `openPorts [80]`;
- control `qa-20261114-1298-wake10k`: the same image with `--env-var WHOAMI_PORT_NUMBER=10000`, `openPorts [10000]`.

**Method:** wait for the `service_hibernated` event (read through the API, so the service is not woken), then make one request with a plain client: `curl -sS -m 400 -D - https://<svc>.onbex.co/?wait=130s`. whoami holds `?wait=130s` for 130 s before answering, and answers 130 s and 400 s holds with 200 when awake (sweep `77c3`).

**The 502** (runs 3–5):

```
HTTP/2 502
content-type: application/json
x-render-origin-server: Render
x-render-routing: no-server

{"error":"bad gateway"}
```

That is the activator's `ErrorHandler` for a proxy error other than a dial failure (`lego/operator/cmd/activator/hold.go:180-192`). A dial failure would be the 504 wake timeout instead. So the activator had reached the woken app, and the exchange then failed while the client's connection to the activator was intact.

**What did not change:**

- the woken pod: one instance (for example `…-s1jci7vk72lofnh2k6ar`, created 14:08:57Z, `Starting up on port 80` at 14:09:08) stayed up through and after the cut, with no restart or replacement;
- no second `service_hibernated` event;
- the bound: w8/329's 100-minute header timeout was live (run 2 proves it);
- the port: discovery is not involved, since the port-10000 control fails the same way.

## What is ruled out (code read)

- **Hold slot:** releasing the hold slot before proxying (`0348c50c6`) only decrements an admission counter (`admit`, `hold.go`), and nothing else reads it.
- **Header timeout:** now 100 minutes (w8/329).
- **Server deadlines:** the held request's server read and write deadlines are cleared (`serve`, `hold.go`).
- **Pod replacement:** the instance list shows the same pod.

## Hypotheses, in the order to check

The activator proxies to the App's cluster Service, `<app>.<ns>.svc:<ServicePort>` (`serviceURL`, `hold.go:112`). Something on that path resets an established connection some seconds after the App turns ready. Candidates:

1. **The wake route switch rewrites the App's Service or its EndpointSlice.** After readiness the operator moves the public Ingress off the activator alias (`app_controller.go`, `ingressRoutesToActivator` and the restore path). If it also patches the App's Service (selector, ports or the image-network Service), the CNI's service load-balancing may reset established flows to the old backend mapping.
2. **The CNI or network policy re-evaluates the activator→pod flow** when the namespace's policies are reconciled after the wake.
3. **The activator's transport** sees `EOF` or a reset from something in between: kube-proxy or Cilium socket LB, or the node.

The timing (12–33 s after readiness, run 2 spared) fits an asynchronous reconcile that sometimes lands after the 130 s.

## Fix (target behavior)

A request held through a wake is served like any other once the app answers, up to w8/329's 100 minutes, as Render serves a spun-down service's first request. A run of the table above answers 200 every time.

Diagnose first:

- the activator's log for the proxy error string of a failing run;
- the App Service's and EndpointSlice's `resourceVersion`/`managedFields` timestamps around the wake;
- the Ingress switch time;
- Cilium or Hubble drops for the activator→pod flow, if available.

Then fix the writer that disturbs established flows, for example by not rewriting an unchanged Service. Moving the route without touching the Service the activator is using is also a fix. As a fallback, the activator could proxy straight to the ready pod's IP (resolved from the Deployment's ready pod) rather than through the Service VIP.

## Regression checks

- **Production:** five fresh runs of the method above (free whoami, hibernated, `?wait=130s`) all answer 200, with the cut-off time recorded. A 10 s and a 60 s wait should also answer 200.
- **Unit or envtest:** whatever writer is found must not rewrite an unchanged Service or EndpointSlice when the App wakes.

- **`w8/216` and `w8/213` (done):** wake-page and routing headers.
