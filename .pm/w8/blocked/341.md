# A live tail opened while a service floods its log shows up to 6,000 lines that history drops: each tail starts its per-instance bucket full, where Render says dropped lines appear in no view

Why: during a flood over Render's 6,000 lines a minute, `bex logs --tail` showed every line for its first 40 s, but `bex logs` for the same window keeps half of them. An owner watching the flood sees lines that are gone when they scroll back. Render says excess lines are dropped and "don't appear in the log explorer or in log streams" (render.com/docs/logging). bex's own comment says the tail keeps the shipper's bucket "or it would show lines history never stored" (`lego/backend/internal/logs/linecap.go:27-29`).

- **Found by:** `/qa-find-bugs-cli w8` routine run, sweep 617d, 2026-11-19 20:05–20:23 UTC. Production pin `1c63b6fe0a34`, which carries w3/m506's `e737b2412`. bex CLI v0.7.0, QA human device login, workspace `bex-canary`.
- **Fixture:** free native Python web service `qa-20261119-617d-py` (`srv-dbvl8vp0rfcc73dnr3s0`, `render-examples/flask-hello-world@main`, deleted, `GET` 404). Start command: `(i=0; end=$((SECONDS+240)); while [ $SECONDS -lt $end ]; do for j in 1 … 20; do i=$((i+1)); echo "FLOOD617 seq=$i"; done; sleep 0.1; done; echo "FLOOD617 end=$i") & exec gunicorn app:app`, about 190 numbered lines a second for 240 s, from 20:08:21 (deploy `dep-dbvld839f6kc73d4aat0`, live 20:08:34).
- **Repro:**
  1. 100 s into the flood: `timeout --preserve-status -s INT 40 bex logs -r <srv> --tail --type app -o json > tail.json` (20:10:00.5–20:10:40.5).
  2. After the flood: page `GET /v1/logs?resource=<srv>&type=app&startTime=<first tail line>&endTime=<last tail line>&direction=forward&limit=100` (`hasMore`/`nextStartTime`) and collect the `seq` values.
- **Evidence (production):**
  - Tail: 7,660 lines, `seq` 17,601–25,260 with none missing, about 190 a second (1,861 / 1,899 / 1,900 / 1,900 per 10 s).
  - History for the same window: 4,048 lines, about 100 a second (1,003 / 1,001 / 991 / 1,003 per 10 s).
  - So 3,612 of the tail's 7,660 lines (47%) are not in history, and every history line was in the tail.
  - The whole flood's history (29,925 lines over 240 s) holds 10,280, 7,699, 6,000 and 5,946 lines in its four minutes. The shipper caps as designed, and the first minute's 10,280 is ADR010's documented token-bucket edge (up to 12,000), not this note.
- **Cause (code):** the tail builds a fresh `capped := lineCap{}` per subscription (`lego/backend/internal/logs/service.go:1861`, in `followPods`), and `admit` creates each instance's bucket with `tokens: appLineBurst` (`linecap.go:49-56`). A tail opened with no start bound follows each pod from one second before it opened (`service.go:1427`). So a tail opened mid-flood admits a fresh 6,000-line burst plus 100 a second, while the shipper's Alloy bucket for that pod has been empty for minutes. ADR010 § Per-instance line cap ("Where history and the tail can differ") and ADR018's application-logs row record this as "Recommendation, not a user decision". Under AGENTS.md's parity rule, an unsigned difference does not stand, and Render's sentence above is the documented behavior.
- **Fix:** warm each instance's tail bucket before it emits. For an app container, open the follow from the tail's start bound minus `2 × appLineBurst / appLineRate` (120 s), pass the lines stamped before the start bound through `capped.admit` without emitting them, and emit from the start bound on. A pod quiet for two minutes then starts full, as today. A pod flooding at 150 lines a second or more starts as empty as the shipper's bucket. Apply the same warm-up to a tail with an explicit `startTime` and to the store-less `readPodLogs` (`service.go:2535-2540`). Bound the warm-up read (it replays up to 120 s of a flooding pod), and keep the filters after the cap. Then delete the "Where history and the tail can differ" exception from ADR010 and ADR018, or narrow it to the clock skew that remains.
- **Blast radius:** `followPods` serves REST `/v1/logs/subscribe` (WebSocket, SSE, NDJSON): the CLI's `--tail`, the dashboard's live viewer (`dashboard/src/features/logs/hooks/use-live-logs.ts`) and the project views' merged tail (`logs/project.go:276`, `logs/rest.go:357-374`). MCP has no tail. Request, datastore and build lines are not capped and must not change.
- **Regression checks:** a `linecap`/`followPods` test where a fake pod has flooded at 200 lines a second for 120 s before a tail opens: the tail admits about 100 a second from its start, failing today with 6,000 more. A quiet pod's tail still passes a 6,000-line burst. On production: the repro above, with tail lines absent from history at most 2% of the tail (clock slack), against 47% today.
- **Unverified:** Render's live-tail behavior during a flood was not observed (no Render account). The argument rests on Render's documented sentence and bex's stated intent.
- **Dedupe:** w3/m506 (blocked on its own production read, t006) shipped the cap and recorded this edge as unverified with "no follow-up filed". `rg 'mid-flood|one more burst'` finds only m506 and the ADRs. Note for w3/m506 t006: this sweep's flood dropped lines for `srv-dbvl8vp0rfcc73dnr3s0`'s pod between 20:08 and 20:12:21 UTC on purpose, so `AppLogLinesRateLimited` or the drop counters may show it.
- **Estimate:** ~1h.

## Shipped (2026-11-19, `a8ef77ce0`)

- **Fix:** an App tail follows each instance from `capWarmUp` (120 s, two refills) before its start bound and passes those lines through the line cap without showing them (`followPods` in `lego/backend/internal/logs/service.go`, constants in `linecap.go`). This covers a tail with no `startTime`, an explicit `startTime` and a `Last-Event-ID` resume. A warming follow drops an unstamped first line, the rest of a line begun before the warm-up. The store-less `readPodLogs` asks the kubelet for `capWarmUpLines` (18,000) more lines and only counts them when the kubelet sends them all. Build, request and datastore tails are unchanged. ADR010 § Per-instance line cap and ADR018's application-logs row narrow the exception to the clock edge and one warm-up edge: a pod that emptied its bucket before the warm-up and then wrote about 100 lines a second all through it can pass at most one burst more than history.
- **Checked:** `TestATailOpenedMidFloodShowsWhatHistoryKeeps` uses a pod that printed 200 lines a second for 5 minutes before the tail. The tail passes what one bucket from the pod's first line keeps (±2), where it passed 11,999 before the fix. A quiet neighbor still passes its 6,000-line burst, and nothing stamped before the bound shows. That test, `TestStorelessHistoryCountsTheFloodBeforeItsLines`, the reworked `TestStorelessHistoryKeepsTheCap` and the warming cases of `TestFollowHoldsAnUnstampedFirstLine` each fail with the fix reverted. The logs package passes under `-race`, `make lint-backend` is clean, and the ship gate's real-DB backend suite passed.
