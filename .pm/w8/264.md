# A running instance that fails its health check is restarted after about two minutes; Render restarts it after 60 seconds

Why: Render restarts an instance once its health check has failed for 60 consecutive seconds, and bex's own Settings help says the same. On bex the kubelet reaches that verdict at 60 s and then runs the pod's 60 s pre-stop drain before it sends `SIGTERM`. A wedged single-instance service is therefore down for about two minutes where Render brings it back in about one. The drain protects requests still arriving at an instance, and this instance left rotation at second 15.

**Severity:** minor (the restart works; the outage of a wedged single-instance service is about twice Render's). **Attribution:** infrastructure/runtime (operator pod projection). **Estimate:** ~2h. **Source:** `/qa-find-bugs-cli` w8 loop sweep `2ba7`, 2026-11-11 UTC, released `bex v0.5.0` (Render CLI v2.35.0), `https://api.bex.co/v1/`, production pin `7ca0227aa477`, workspace `bex-canary`, QA human device login.

## Evidence

One free native Node web service with a health check path. Its server logs every health request and can be told to answer 500, or to answer 200 after 8 s:

```text
$ bex services create --name qa-20261111-2ba7-hc --type web_service --repo https://github.com/bex-co/bex --branch main \
    --runtime node --root-directory examples/static-site --plan free --build-command true \
    --start-command "<server with /healthz, /bad and /slow?ms=8000>" --health-check-path /healthz \
    --auto-deploy=false -o json --confirm
```

Times are UTC. Health requests and `SIGTERM` are from `bex logs -r <srv> --type app`; the public URL was polled every second.

| | Health answers 500 | Health answers 200 after 8 s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First failing check | 14:26:58.5 | 14:29:54.1 |
| Public URL stops answering 200 | 14:27:09 | 14:30:11 |
| Sixth failed 10 s check (the restart verdict) | 14:27:48.4 | 14:30:53.3 |
| `SIGTERM` in the app log | 14:28:48.5 | 14:31:53.3 |
| Restarted process listening | 14:28:50.2 | 14:31:55.1 |
| Public URL answers 200 again | 14:29:04 | 14:32:11 |
| First failing check → `SIGTERM` | 110 s | 119 s |
| Public outage | 115 s | 120 s |

- The log shows two series of checks: one every 5 s and one every 10 s. The 10 s series stops after its sixth failure. The 5 s series continues for another 60 s, and then `SIGTERM` arrives: 60.1 s after the sixth failure in the first run, 60.0 s in the second.
- The events are right for this case: `server_failed` with `reason.unhealthy` at 14:27:12Z and 14:30:24Z, `server_available` at 14:29:03Z and 14:32:11Z.
- `bex services instances <srv>` lists the same instance id before and after. The container restarts in place.

**The same drain on an instance that never served.** In the cancel runs filed as w8/265, a deploy whose start command sleeps 45 s was canceled at 14:54:20.5. Its instance never became Ready. Its pod was removed at about 14:54:36, yet the process went on to log `LISTEN` at 14:54:56 and received `SIGTERM` at 14:55:35.8.

Production rolled to pin `f609163ada8e` at 14:48–14:49 UTC. The two health-check runs are from before the roll and the canceled instance is from after it. The probe and drain code cited below is the same at both pins.

## Expected

Render, [Health checks](https://render.com/docs/health-checks):

- "If a running service instance fails consecutive health checks for 15 seconds, Render temporarily stops routing traffic to it to give it an opportunity to recover."
- "If an instance fails consecutive health checks for 60 seconds, Render automatically restarts the instance."

bex says the same in the Health Check Path help ("60 seconds of consecutive failures restarts the instance", `dashboard/src/features/services/locales/en.ts:1339`) and in `docs/ADR004-app-deployment.md` ("60s of consecutive failures restarts the instance").

So the process gets `SIGTERM` about 60 s after its first failing check, then its shutdown delay as today.

## Root cause

- The restart window is right. `livenessRestartWindowSeconds = 60` gives the liveness probe six failures at a 10 s period (`lego/operator/internal/controller/deployment_projection.go:104`, `:147`, `:154`, `:377-382`; w7/m81).
- `applyDrain` (`deployment_projection.go:531-538`) gives every web and private service container `preStop: sleep 60` (`drainSeconds`, `:493`). w1/m154 added it at 10 s and w8/180 raised it to 60 s, Render's time for the original instance to finish in-flight requests after a deploy switches traffic.
- The kubelet runs a container's pre-stop hook before every kill it starts, a liveness kill included, and sends `SIGTERM` when the hook returns. The restart therefore costs the 60 s window plus the 60 s drain. The 60.0 s and 60.1 s gaps above are that hook.
- The instance being killed stopped receiving requests when its readiness probe failed three times at 5 s (w8/189), 45 s before the verdict. It has nothing to drain. Nor does a pod that was never Ready.

History: w4/196 measured 77 s end to end on 2026-10-04, when the drain was 10 s. w8/189 measured "t+109 s" on 2026-10-22, the day the drain became 60 s, and read it as roughly tracking Render's window.

## Fix

The drain should run only for an instance that may still be receiving requests. `preStop.sleep` cannot tell, so the hook has to ask.

1. Replace the sleep with an exec of bex's own `health-probe` binary, which an init container already installs into an `emptyDir` for services with a Health Check Path (`lego/operator/internal/controller/health_probe.go:38-106`). Add a `drain` subcommand: run the service's check once (HTTP with the path, a TCP connect without one); return at once if it fails, otherwise sleep `drainSeconds`.
2. Install the binary for every drained web and private pod, not only those with a path.
3. Keep `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` at drain plus shutdown delay, and keep the lazy adoption rule: a stored template takes the new hook on its next release, never by an operator upgrade rolling running services.
4. `livenessProbe.terminationGracePeriodSeconds` is not a fix on its own. It would cut the hook short, but the grace period it sets also covers the time after `SIGTERM`, so the process would lose its shutdown delay. Check the kubelet's behaviour before relying on it.

One edge to decide: an instance whose check is failing at the moment its pod is deleted for another reason (a deploy, a scale-in) would also skip the drain. That is the instance Render has already stopped routing to.

## Acceptance

- A free web service whose health path starts answering 500: the app log shows `SIGTERM` 60–75 s after the first failing check, and the public URL answers 200 again within about 90 s.
- The same for a health path that answers after 8 s.
- A deploy of a healthy service is unchanged: the original instance gets `SIGTERM` about 60 s after the new one takes traffic, and a 40 s request in flight on it completes (w8/180).
- A canceled deploy's instance that never became Ready is gone within seconds of its pod's removal.
- Envtest: the container's pre-stop hook is the drain command, the grace period is unchanged, and a stored template with the old hook is left alone until its next release.

## Unverified

- The kubelet's hook handling was not read on the cluster. The exact 60 s gap in both runs is the evidence.
- Whether Render sends `SIGTERM` and honours the shutdown delay on a health-check restart. Its docs say only "restarts the instance".
- A service with no health check path (TCP check) was not measured. Its projection is the same.
