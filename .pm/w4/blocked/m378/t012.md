## Objective

DoD 6's truncation bullet holds on production: `select g, repeat('x', 100000) as big from generate_series(1,60) g` (6 MB) returns the rows that fit in 5 MB of JSON, then a second text item that starts `Results truncated: showing the first <n> rows.`, with no `isError`. No request of this size may take bex-api down for other callers.

## Found by the closeout replay (QA loop round 408, 2026-11-15 09:47–09:49 UTC, pin `89ac87445bec`, which carries t011)

- **Fixture:** a Free Postgres `qa-20261115-r408-pg` (`dpg-dbsntv6oou4c73fdrngg`), created through MCP `create_postgres` in `bex-canary`. It was deleted afterwards (`GET` 404).
- **Calls:** streamable HTTP to `https://api.bex.co/mcp` with a session cookie, `tools/call query_render_postgres`.
  - `select 1 as a, 'x' as b, null::int as c` returned `[{"a":1,"b":"x","c":null}]`.
  - `… where false` returned `[]`.
  - The 600-row `generate_series` returned 600 rows.
  - `repeat('x', 300000)` returned its one row (300,000 characters).
  - A write returned `isError: true` ("read-only transaction; write and DDL statements are not permitted"). `pg_sleep(120)` returned `isError: true` ("query exceeded the statement timeout").
  - **The 6 MB query, sent twice a minute apart, came back as the body `error code: 502`** (Cloudflare's edge page) both times.
  - The call right after the first one also came back with no JSON-RPC body.
  - Plain `GET /v1/users` answered `200` at 09:49:01, :03 and :05, about 10 s after the second.
- **Not repeated:** w4/671 asks that large-payload probes stay off production, so the query was not sent a third time. bex-api pod restarts in that window were not read.

## Reading (code read; the cause was not seen)

- `collectRenderRows` and `renderQueryRows` (`lego/backend/internal/postgres/query_render.go:79-128`) stop at 5 MB of row JSON, and the wire cap is sized to the same budget. On paper the answer is about 5 MB of rows plus the notice, encoded twice (once as the text item, once in the JSON-RPC body).
- An edge 502 means the upstream connection failed mid-request. Candidates:
  1. bex-api dying or being OOM-killed while it buffers and re-encodes the ~5 MB answer. That is w4/671's open hypothesis, with large secret-file writes there.
  2. A per-request write timeout, or an SSE write that stalls on a single ~5 MB `data:` line.
  3. Traefik or Cloudflare refusing an unusually large single SSE event.

## Steps

1. Read production's bex-api restarts and `OOMKilled` reasons for 09:47–09:49 UTC on 2026-11-15, alongside w4/671's window (ops access).
2. On the mock cluster, send the same call and watch bex-api's memory, its restarts and Traefik's answer. Then try 3 MB (untruncated) and 5.5 MB to separate "large answer" from "truncation path".
3. Fix what it shows. Stream the rows into the response instead of building them twice; bound the per-request memory; or chunk the SSE event. Make sure a failure fails only that call, never the process.
4. Test: an e2e or kind check that the 6 MB call answers the truncated rows plus the notice, and that a concurrent `GET /v1/users` stays `200`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On production, the 6 MB call returns the truncated rows and the notice, with no `isError`, and other calls keep answering throughout.
- [ ] If step 1 shows bex-api restarted, w4/671 is updated with that evidence.
