## Objective

Replace `count(avg_over_time(container_memory_working_set_bytes{…}[w]))*w` with a running-time measure.

## Context

- One option: per pod (deduplicate containers with `max by (pod)` or `count by (pod)`), take the number of scrapes in the window × the scrape interval, i.e. `sum(count by (pod) (count_over_time(container_memory_working_set_bytes{…}[w])) * scrape_interval)`. Equivalently, use a pod-phase or presence series.
- Keep the source-health gates ADR023 requires: reject the window on missing scrapes rather than invent seconds.
- `lego/backend/internal/usage/service.go:1113-1131`; callers `:683` (datastores) and `:966` (Apps).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Unit/fake-Prometheus tests: a 300 s pod gives ≈300; a two-container pod is counted once; old/new pods during a rollout count their own lifetimes; a full-hour single pod gives 3600.

## Outcome (2026-10-04)

Done. `usage.instanceSecondsQuery` evaluates `sum(count_over_time((max by (pod) (container_memory_working_set_bytes{…}))[3599s:15s]))` and multiplies by 15 s:
- per-pod presence, with a pod's containers collapsed into one;
- the cAdvisor scrape step;
- a range one second short so Prometheus 2.x's aligned left endpoint isn't double-counted.

It serves both callers (Apps and datastores). The source-health gates are unchanged. Verified in Prometheus v2.54.1's engine (`promtool test rules`, deployed chart line): 5 min reads 285, Valkey with its exporter sidecar reads 3600 (the old query gave 7200), two replicas read 7200, a rollout with a 2-minute overlap reads 3720 (old 7200), and the old query gives 3600 for the 5-minute pod.
