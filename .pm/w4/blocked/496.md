## Triage (loopx w4, 2026-11-05)

Read from code; no production data was available to this session.

- **Coverage arithmetic is not wrong.** `store/usage.go` `evaluateStreamCoverage` stops a stream's watermark at the first expected hour with no healthy health row, and `aggregateUsageCoverage` takes the minimum over active streams. One unrecorded hour early in the month therefore pins `through` there until the month rolls over, by design ("undercount, never invent").
- **Hours are not retried after 48 h.** `usage/service.go` `catchupLimit` is 48 h, and catch-up fills gaps after a meter's latest recorded window, so an hour that was skipped while later hours succeeded is not re-metered (the w4/442 note says the same for October).
- **Most likely cause:** the three workspaces' pinned hours (`2026-11-01T00:00Z`, `T02:00Z`) fall before the w4/442 fix (`5c1607804`) reached production. Until then `egressquery.Health` gated `direct` fleet-wide and degraded 40–55% of hours, so early-November hours were skipped for every app. The same note recorded that already-degraded hours stay degraded. This fits the observation that `through` does not move after later deploys. It does not explain why `http`, `instance`, `postgres` and `websocket` are also degraded in those hours; that needs the per-stream health rows.
- **Nothing to fix in code until the data is read.** The two questions the note asks (missing hours per source per workspace; whether usage rows or Stripe meter events are short) need a read-only production DB session: `psql "$DATABASE_URL" -v ws=<tea-…> -f scripts/usage-coverage-diagnose.sql` for the three workspaces.
- **Decision for the user after that read:** (1) re-meter the degraded November hours that Prometheus still retains (a one-off repair that writes `usage_hourly` rows for past hours, so it changes billable usage and needs approval), or (2) accept `partial` through 2026-11-30 and rely on the 442 fix keeping later months complete.
