## Objective

In canonical mode with IEXTEN, implement VLNEXT (Ctrl-V) and VREPRINT (Ctrl-R), and echo a newline under ECHONL even with ECHO off, as Linux does:

- Ctrl-V, Ctrl-C queues a literal `\x03` and sends no signal.
- Ctrl-R reprints the pending line.
- With `stty -echo echonl`, a typed Enter still echoes `\n`.

## Context

- Linux `drivers/tty/n_tty.c` `n_tty_receive_char_special()`, `if (ldata->icanon)` block:
  - **LNEXT** (with IEXTEN) sets `ldata->lnext`. With ECHO and ECHOCTL it echoes `^` then `\b`. The next byte goes through `n_tty_receive_char_lnext()`, which queues it literally, even a signal, erase or newline character.
  - **REPRINT** (with ECHO and IEXTEN) echoes the character, then `\n`, then the pending line from `canon_head`.
  - **`\n`** is echoed when ECHO or ECHONL is set.
- `n_tty_set_termios()` puts LNEXT, REPRINT and EOL2 in `char_map` only under ICANON plus IEXTEN, and REPRINT also needs ECHO.
- gVisor (fork `bex/echo-signal-chars`): `transform` (~:579-770) has no LNEXT state. Literal-next is the one gap here that needs a field on `queue` or `lineDiscipline` that survives across writes. Linux keeps `lnext` across `receive_buf` calls, so a test writes `\x16` and `\x03` in two separate writes as well as in one. The echo tail (~:745-760) echoes only under ECHO.

## Steps

1. Implement each, guarded by t001's disabled-character helper.
2. Add syscall tests in `test/syscalls/linux/pty.cc`:
   - LNEXT then VINTR in one write and in two, with no signal and `\x03` read.
   - LNEXT then VERASE queues `\x7f`.
   - VREPRINT's echo bytes.
   - `-echo echonl` echoes `\n` and nothing else.
   - In noncanonical mode, each of these bytes is queued as is.
3. Run them natively, on runsc, and on runsc with the fix reverted.
