## Objective

The wildcard route lives in a child Ingress, `<app>.wildcard`, beside the serving one, as the www/apex redirect hosts live in `<app>-host-redirects`. Neither was in the App's router set, so bandwidth, egress usage and (since w4/559) every request metric left their traffic out, and a wildcard-only App, which has no serving Ingress, failed those reads. The request log labelled the wildcard route's lines with the child Ingress's name, so the App's Logs and request counts missed them.

## Acceptance criteria

- `core.TraefikRouterNames` returns the routers of every Ingress the App owns: its serving Ingress (unless another App controls that name) and each child it controls (`v1alpha1.ChildIngressNames`), from one List, deduplicated. An App named like another's child meters none of the other's routers.
- A wildcard-only App's reads resolve.
- The log shipper attributes a `<app>.wildcard` Ingress's lines to the App; an App whose own name ends in `-host-redirects` keeps its name.
- The operator and bex-api share the child names through `lego/types`.

## Result

- **2026-11-11 (loopx w4):** done. Found while reviewing w4/559. `v1alpha1.ChildIngressName`/`ChildIngressNames` replace the operator's local helper; `TraefikRouterNames` lists the namespace once and keeps only Ingresses the App controls (`appIngress`); the shipper strips only `.wildcard`. Tests: `TestTraefikRouterNamesIncludeTheAppsChildIngresses` (serving + redirect + wildcard union, an App named like a child, a wildcard-only App beside an unowned look-alike) and two `test_log_shipper.py` cases. Residuals recorded in ADR005 § Wildcard domains: the redirect Ingress's request-log lines keep its name (its suffix could be another App's), and a `wildcard.<base>` host beside `*.<base>` collides on one Traefik router key.
