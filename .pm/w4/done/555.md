# 555 — After a quick Suspend then Resume, the Events feed's newest entry reads "Service suspended" while the service is running: the `service_suspended` + `service_resumed` pair share one timestamp and sort in the wrong order

Why: the newest event is how an owner reads "what state is my service in". After a Resume, the top entry says the service was suspended "now". It sits above "Service resumed", and the header says Running. The pair also carries the time of a later observation pass, not the moments the owner acted.

- **Source / severity / estimate:** live `/qa-find-bugs w4` loop, round 41, 2026-11-10 20:38–20:43 UTC (`muse.env`, workspace `bex-canary`, pin `c12cd57facb1`). **Minor** (event order and time; no outage). ~30m. A residual of [w4/527](done/527.md).
- **Fixture:** Free image service `qa-20261110-ev41` (`srv-dbpo1blme2kc73f2lfig`, `nginx:1.27-alpine`). Deleted (GET 404).
- **Probe (reproduced twice):**
  - **Run 1:** `POST …/restart` at 20:39:30, `POST …/suspend` at 20:39:56 (202), `POST …/resume` at 20:40:11 (202). `GET …/events?limit=20`, newest first:
    1. `service_suspended @20:40:28`
    2. `service_resumed @20:40:28`
    3. `suspender_removed @20:40:11`
    4. `deploy_ended @20:39:58`, canceled: "Canceled: the service was suspended"
    5. `suspender_added @20:39:56`
  - **Run 2:** `suspend` at 20:42:23, `resume` at 20:42:32. The feed read `service_suspended @20:42:42` and `service_resumed @20:42:42` (newest first), above `suspender_removed @20:42:32` and `suspender_added @20:42:24`.
  - **Dashboard:** the Events tab listed "Service suspended · now" first and "Service resumed · now" second. Meanwhile the header read "Service Running", REST `suspended: "not_suspended"`, and there was 1 instance.
- **Root cause:**
  - **w4/527's fix emits the pair in one pass.** For "the flag rose and fell while the phase stayed up", `observedStateFacts` appends `makeFact("suspended", …)` and `makeFact("resumed", …)` together (`lego/backend/internal/store/event_facts.go:610-612`).
    - Both take `At: obs.At`, the pass that noticed the Resume.
    - Their source keys differ only by the suffix: `observed:<app>:suspended:<nanos>` vs `…:resumed:<nanos>`.
  - **The feed's sort puts the wrong one on top.** It orders `at DESC, key DESC` (`lego/backend/internal/store/events.go:500`). With equal `at`, "suspended" > "resumed" decides the order. So the suspension always lists as the newer event.
  - **The test checks only array order.** `event_facts_test.go:202-214` asserts the facts' slice order, not their timestamps, so it passes.
- **Fix (target behavior):**
  - The pair keeps chronological order on every surface. `service_suspended` is stamped no later than when the suspension was first recorded: the checkpoint's `updated_at`, read as `previousChangedAt` at `event_facts.go:391-394` and written at `:455`, which is the pass that first saw `spec.suspended=true`. `service_resumed` keeps `obs.At`.
  - When those times collide, `resumed` is at least 1 µs later. Postgres `timestamptz` keeps microseconds, so a nanosecond offset would be lost.
  - The REST, GraphQL and MCP events lists and the dashboard then read, newest first: `service_resumed` → `service_suspended` → `suspender_removed`/`suspender_added` in their real order.
  - Extend the test to assert `suspended.At < resumed.At`, and that the feed lists `resumed` first.
- **Blast radius:**
  - Only the quick-undo branch at `:610-612` changes.
  - The two phase-edge branches (`:613-637`) already stamp distinct passes, and their tests stay as regression controls.
  - The same fact types feed push (`notifications/push_worker.go:749-751`) and webhook fan-out (`webhooks/service.go:234-235`). Their order follows the fact `At`.
- **Unverified:**
  - Whether webhook receivers saw `service_suspended` after `service_resumed`. No webhook was subscribed this round.
  - Whether the service actually stopped serving during the 9–15 s suspension. I didn't probe the URL during the window.
  - The datastore suspend path, which 527 also left unchecked.
- **Render:** Suspend and Resume each produce their own event, however quickly they follow each other (527). Render's events are in time order.
- **Dedupe:**
  - w4/527 (done) introduced the pair and fixed its emission, not its order or time.
  - w4/508 (done) and w4/m230 (done) cover the other lifecycle windows.
  - `grep -rliE "service_suspended.{0,80}(order|same timestamp|newest)" .pm` finds nothing open.
  - Not deploy lag: the only change to `event_facts.go` in `c12cd57facb1..origin/main` is w3/m317's event details, not this branch.
- **DoD (live):** on an owned Free web service, `POST …/suspend` then `POST …/resume` within ~10 s.
  - `GET …/events` lists `service_resumed` above `service_suspended`, and the two timestamps differ.
  - `service_suspended` is no later than the first observation of the suspension.
  - The dashboard Events tab's top entry is "Service resumed".

- **2026-11-11 (loopx w4):** fixed: `quickSuspendPair` stamps `service_suspended` at the checkpoint's last change (the pass that recorded the suspension) and keeps `service_resumed` at the pass that noticed the Resume, at least 1 µs later; source keys keep the noticing pass, so replays stay no-ops. The newest-first feed (REST, GraphQL, MCP, dashboard) now reads `service_resumed` above `service_suspended`. Unit test asserts both timestamps; `TestPGQuickSuspendPairListsResumeOnTop` asserts the real feed order and times. Push and webhook delivery already followed insertion order (`recorded_at`), which was right.
