# Price a cron minute at Render's per-minute rate × 0.70, not at the compute rate over 730 hours

Why: ADR030 promises Render's prices less 30%, and w4/m690's guard found that a bex cron minute is Render's × 0.70 × 720/730, 1.4% under that promise, because Render prices a cron minute over a 30-day month and bex over 730 hours.

- **Render (render.com/pricing § Compute > Cron Jobs, read 2026-11-26; `lego/backend/internal/pricing/testdata/render-cron-prices.tsv`):** each row is the same plan's monthly compute price over 43,200 minutes, shown to five decimals: `0.5c-512mb` $7 → $0.00016, `2c-4g` $85 → $0.00197, `8c-32g` $450 → $0.01042. render.com/docs/cronjobs: "Billing is prorated by the second, based on active running time".
- **bex today:** a cron run meters `instance_seconds` at its compute tier's `pricing.yaml` rate, derived over 730 hours (`MonthSeconds`). Starter's minute is $4.90 / 43,800 = $0.000112 against Render's $0.00016 × 0.70 = $0.000112 (0.1% under), and Pro's $59.50 / 43,800 = $0.001358 against $0.00197 × 0.70 = $0.001379 (1.5% under). `cronTier` in `price_table_test.go` holds this relation, and ADR030 §9 and §18 record it.
- **Look at:** pricing a cron job's `instance_seconds` at Render's per-minute rate × 0.70 without a new Stripe Price (the catalog is at Stripe's 20 items per Subscription): export a cron job's seconds on its tier's meter scaled by 730/720, as sandbox CPU is exported on `sandbox_compute_seconds` as the units that cost the same (ADR023), or a cron rate per tier on the sheet. The New Cron Job form's per-minute rate (`InstanceUSDPerMinute`), the usage estimate and the cron minimum's run charge (§18) must agree with what is billed. A cron price rise is a change customers will see: say so in the result.
- **Done when:** a bex cron minute on every tier equals Render's per-minute price × 0.70 within its display rounding, `cronTier` checks that directly, and ADR030 §9 and §18 drop the 720/730 note.
