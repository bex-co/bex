`serviceEvents(type:"cron_job_run_ended")` → `[]` at 09:21:20, five minutes after two runs had finished.

**The timestamp is what settles it.** The single `cron_job_run_started` fires at 09:16:06 — the exact instant of the `runCronJob` call, not of the run's execution — and the scheduled run that demonstrably executed (it logged at 09:20:01) produced nothing. That is the pre-m118 `apps.TriggerCronRun → cron_job_run_started` mapping still in force, and it is precisely the defect m118 describes: _"the feed showed a manual trigger and nothing else: a schedule that started and failed on its own was invisible in Activity."_

The trap worth recording: the type **name** `cron_job_run_started` is identical before and after m118, so seeing one in the feed does **not** prove the fix is live. Only the timestamp (trigger-time vs run-time) and the scheduled run's absence distinguish them.

## Audit log: the same authorize-time shape, but deliberate — do not file it as m122's twin

While checking the above, `auditLogs` was found to record refused operations as successes: the two refusals above produced `apps.AddDomain … status success` and `apps.SetSourceAndRegistryCredential … status success`, alongside a genuinely-successful `UpdateServiceNameEvent … success`, all at 23:37:18Z with `targetName: null`. 100 consecutive entries were `success`; no `denied` row appeared for any refusal.

This looks like m122's defect on a second surface, but it is **not the same thing and should not be filed as one.** `audit/graphql.go:58-67` and `audit/service.go:169` define the field as a binary *authorization* outcome — `denied` when OpenFGA refused, `success` otherwise — with a comment explaining that GraphQL keeps the richer `denied` value while REST maps it onto Render's closed `success|error` enum. A validation refusal is authorized-then-rejected, so `success` is what the field is designed to say.

Worth a product decision rather than a bug report: for a forensic surface, "the caller was allowed to try" and "the thing happened" are different claims, and an `apps.AddDomain / success / target null` row states the weaker one in the stronger one's words. If anyone picks that up, note that `targetName: null` on all three rows makes it worse — the row does not even name what was touched.
