## Objective

Know, from production Loki, which part of a day's raw lines a prefilter can drop, and what each candidate LogQL line filter costs, before writing one.

## Context

- Production read access: `~/.local/toolchains/bin` (kubectl) on `PATH`, `bash scripts/fetch-app-kubeconfig.sh <path outside the repo>`, then `kubectl -n monitoring port-forward svc/loki 13100:3100`. The port-forward adds about 0.17 s per sequential request and carries 3–5 MB/s, so compare requests, Loki's `execTime` and MB, not wall time (w5/m503 Findings).
- `lego/backend/internal/logs/loki_timing_test.go` (build tag `lokitiming`) reads through `NewLokiSource` as `QueryLogs` hands it a query; its targets come from the environment and are never committed. Never write tenant identifiers into the board or a commit: name services as "the busiest" and "an idle" one.
- Candidates: `|= "<variant>"` per case variant (only for very short terms), `|~ "<explicit class sequence>"` such as `[eE][rR][rR]` with `[iI\x{0130}]` and `[kK\x{212A}]`, `|~ "(?i)…"` (not proven a superset, time it only for comparison), and for request streams `| json … | line_format …` followed by a line filter.

## Steps

1. Over one day of the busiest service, count lines by `type` (request, app, build, Postgres, Key Value) and the raw bytes of each.
2. Time a no-match and a common-match term over one hour and one day with each candidate filter, per type.
3. Record the numbers and the chosen candidate in the milestone README's Findings.
