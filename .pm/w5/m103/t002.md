## Objective

Make "member" mean a person on every read surface: exclude machine bindings from the member list, and compute seat usage over the same set the `CanAddMember` refusal uses — so the displayed count and the refusal can never disagree (the invariant ADR024 states for seat usage).

## Context

- The single source is `store.ListTenantMembers` (`lego/backend/internal/store/workspaces.go:209`) plus the seat formula (`members/service.go:472`, `store.CanAddMember`). Filter once, at the store, so every consumer agrees — `workspaces.ListMembers` (the Render owners API) reads the same rows.
- Whatever discriminator t001 settles on (subject kind, or an explicit column) must be deterministic — no email-shape guessing.
- If a column is the right answer, it is a migration: follow the numbered migration chain in `internal/store/migrations/`, verified up and down against real Postgres (the w5/m74 precedent).
- Do not change what `identityResolved: false` means for humans (w4/070) — unresolved humans stay listed and removable.

## Steps

1. Implement the discriminator decided in t001.
2. Filter machine rows out of the member list and the seat count in one place; verify `workspaces.ListMembers`, `members.List`, `SeatUsage` and `CanAddMember` all follow.
3. If a migration is needed, write up and down, and verify both against real Postgres.
4. Confirm the Team page count, the "X of Y seats" title and the invite refusal all agree on a workspace with bound keys.

## Files

- `lego/backend/internal/store/workspaces.go`, `members.go`, `migrations/`
- `lego/backend/internal/members/service.go`

- [ ] Member lists on all three surfaces and the Team page show only people.
- [ ] Seat usage and the invite refusal are computed over the same set.
- [ ] Any migration applies and rolls back cleanly against real Postgres.
- [ ] Unresolved human members are unaffected.
