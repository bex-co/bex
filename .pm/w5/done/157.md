# A locked App patch that re-reads after a conflict can apply a deleted service's write to its namesake

Why: `rollout.PatchAppLocked` re-reads the App by name after a conflicting patch and runs the mutation again. If the service was deleted and recreated in between, the retry mutates the new service. A secrets write then projects the dead write's map as the new App: `confirmLiveApp` passes, because the App it holds is now live. The new service's adopted Secret takes the dead service's values, and the dead write answers OK.

- **Where:** `lego/backend/internal/rollout/rollout.go` `PatchAppLocked` (:169-186; the re-read is at :182). Callers that project inside the mutation: secrets `materializeEnv` (`service.go` :1059), `materializeFiles` (`files.go` :629) and `batch.go` :297. Env groups also patch linked Apps through it (`envgroups/service.go` :1195, :1233).
- **Fix:** remember the App's UID before the loop. After a re-read that returns another UID, refuse with `core.ErrConflict` (the service was replaced) instead of mutating it.
- **Test:** a secrets write whose App is deleted and recreated, with an adopted namesake, between its projection and its App patch. The write refuses, and the namesake's Secret keeps the create's data. The reproduction is w5/147's review probe `TestProbeLockedPatchReReadSwapsInNamesake`, kept at `/tmp/w5-157/probe_test.go` for this session. A rollout-level test should also show a UID change after the re-read refusing without calling the mutation again.
- Found by w5/147's quality review (2026-10-07).

## Result (2026-10-07)

- `core.RereadApp` refreshes an App from the API server. When an App with another UID now holds the name, the service's namesake after a delete and recreate, it answers the new `core.ErrServiceReplaced` (409, "the service changed since this request read it") and leaves the caller's App as it was. It replaces secrets' private `errServiceReplaced`.
- Every locked re-read of an App by name now goes through it:
  - `rollout.PatchAppLocked`'s re-read after a conflict, which serves secrets writes and env-group fan-outs;
  - `deploys.openRelease`'s read under the trigger lock;
  - `writeDeployHookToken`'s re-read after a conflict, so a rotation can't rotate the namesake's hook;
  - `store.markReleaseCanceled`'s re-read, so a cancel can't stamp the namesake.
- `openRelease` drops its hand-copied optimistic-lock loop for `PatchAppLocked`: same attempts, one fewer read when it gives up.
- `secrets.compensateEnvironment` handles a replaced service's write differently now. It still takes back a change its store write merged into the namesake's map while that write is the latest. It projects nothing and answers the replacement instead of `ENVIRONMENT_RESTORATION_FAILED`.
- Tests:
  - rollout `TestPatchLockedLeavesARecreatedNamesakeAlone`;
  - deploys `TestADeployForARecreatedServiceLeavesItsNamesakeAlone` and `TestARotationLeavesARecreatedNamesakesDeployHookAlone`;
  - store `TestCancelReleaseLeavesARecreatedNamesakeAlone`; w5/136's cancel test now passes the caller's App with its UID;
  - secrets `TestAWriteRetriedAfterItsServiceWasRecreatedLeavesTheNamesakeAlone` and `TestAReplacedWritesMergeIntoItsNamesakesMapIsTakenBack`.
- Ten mutants fail the tests: no UID check, swapping the App before refusing, and plain re-reads in the patch loop, the trigger lock, the hook rotation and the cancel. Also no take-back, the replacement not detected, the superseded answer, and an off-by-one release generation.
- Docs: ADR013, ADR018 (concurrent triggers) and ADR060 (cancel).
- Follow-up: w5/158. Env-group fan-outs should treat a replaced linked service as a stale link, not a failure. The CAS-path compensation note went to w5/156.
