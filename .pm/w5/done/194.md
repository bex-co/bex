# 194 — The disk meter never re-meters an hour behind its newest row, so a single disk's missed hour is skipped for good

Why: 2026-11-17 06:00Z went unmetered for 7 disks, `UsageHourNearlyUnmeterable` (disk) paged critical for 24 hours, and the hour passed its 48-hour window unbilled at about 2026-11-19 07:00Z, because nothing could re-meter it: the alert's runbook line "the next hourly pass meters the hour" is false for disk.

- **Evidence (production, read-only, 2026-11-19):** `min by (source) (bex_usage_oldest_unmetered_hour_timestamp_seconds)` was 2026-11-17T06:00:00Z for `disk` and 0 for every other source. `usage_hourly` holds rows at 05:00Z and 07:00Z but none at 06:00Z for `srv-dbko6q88rods73dicapg` (15 GB) and `srv-dbko6rg8rods73dicarg` (1 GB) in a billed workspace: 16 GB-hours, well under a cent. Five more disks in billing-excluded workspaces have no 06:00Z row either.
- **Cause:** `catchUpDisksThrough` (`lego/backend/internal/usage/service.go`) resumes at `store.LatestUsageWindowForKind` + 1h, the newest disk row of any disk. Once any later window is metered, an earlier hole is never revisited. w6/132 made each window commit whole, but it reached production at 2026-11-17 09:41Z (pin `5bcfe8939f5b`), three hours after this hole formed, and its backfill covered 02:00Z only. Any future hole of one disk's stream behind the cursor has the same fate.
- **Fix:** start each pass at the disk source's oldest hole inside the catch-up window (`store.PGStore.UnmeteredUsageHoles` already finds it per stream) and re-meter forward from there. `meterDiskWindow` is idempotent over committed size periods, so re-metering a metered window is safe.
- **Done when:** a test leaves one disk's window empty behind a newer metered window and the next pass fills it; 2026-11-17 06:00Z is recorded as an accepted undercount in the `bex` November coverage, as 2026-11-08 13:00Z was (w6/132). ~1h.

**Done 2026-11-19 (w5 `/loopx`).**

- **Fix:** `catchUpDisksThrough` (`lego/backend/internal/usage/service.go`) starts a pass at the disk source's oldest hole inside the catch-up window (`oldestFillableHoles`, the unmetered-hour gauge's own read of `store.UnmeteredUsageHoles`) when it is older than the newest disk row, and re-meters forward from it in order. A failed hole read is logged and the pass meters on from its cursor. ADR082 D9 records the rule.
- **Tests:** `TestDiskMeterFillsAHoleBehindANewerWindowPG` (real Postgres) commits one disk's row of a window without the other's, meters the next window whole, checks that `UnmeteredUsageHoles` reports the missing disk, then runs the next pass and finds every disk metered at every window and no disk hole. `TestDiskMeterFillsAHoleBehindANewerWindow` checks that the pass re-meters the hole and each later window in order and ignores another source's older hole, and `TestDiskMeterMetersOnWhenTheHoleReadFails` checks that a failed hole read still meters the new hour. Both hole tests fail with the hole start disabled.
- **Production read (2026-11-19 15:36Z, read-only):** the 06:00Z hole is two disks, not seven: the 15 GB and 1 GB `bex` disks named above, 57,600 GB-seconds. The window holds the other four live disks' rows. Three more disks in billing-excluded workspaces had rows at 05:00Z and none at 06:00Z, but they were deleted between 05:34Z and 05:37Z and owe nothing there; their streams end at 06:00Z. A recompute of every window from now − 49 h finds no missing or differing disk row, so nothing else needs a backfill.
- **Accepted undercount:** 2026-11-17 06:00Z for those two `bex` disks (16 GB-hours, 57,600 GB-seconds) left the 48 h window at about 07:00Z today and stays an undercount in `bex`'s November coverage, as 2026-11-08 13:00Z did (w6/132).
