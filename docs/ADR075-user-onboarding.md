# ADR075 — User onboarding: an actionable first-run experience

**Status:** Proposed (2026-08-19); corrected 2026-08-20 after a PM/principal-engineer review — the earlier draft's "D8 implemented in w6/m43" claim was wrong (no such milestone existed and the Kratos config was unshipped); the D7+D8 launch gates shipped via `w6/m42` (closed 2026-08-21): **both gates are live on production** — `BEX_REQUIRE_PAYMENT_METHOD=all` with every first-party workspace Mode-A excluded or bound, and the D8 hybrid Kratos config (password + OIDC) with branded courier emails; D1–D6 remain proposed. **D8 revised again 2026-08-20 (user decision, after walking the implemented flow on dev-6):** the wall moved from the door to a seamless in-product step — registration mints a session, verification continues in place, and the login refusal remains only as the backstop for stale unverified accounts (see D8). Source: PM review of the dashboard sign-up/onboarding experience (2026-08-19, `/pm-brainstorm`). Composes with [ADR012](ADR012-auth.md) (Kratos identity — unchanged), [ADR024](ADR024-members.md) (invites), and [ADR046](ADR046-payment-onboarding-and-paid-gating.md) (payment onboarding). **Revises ADR046 (2026-08-19, user decision):** signup is no longer card-free — a bound payment method is required before any resource usage (free tier included), collected as a first-class onboarding step; ADR046's §1 card-free decision and its "mandatory card at signup — rejected" alternative are superseded, while its mechanism (setup-mode Checkout + local `payment_method_bound_at` marker + `PaymentGate`) is reused unchanged.

---

## Context

The auth **machinery** is mature: the full Kratos flow set ships (sign-up, login, verification, recovery, MFA), the workspace is auto-minted on first login (`lego/backend/internal/api/tenancy.go:142`), invite redemption is secure-by-default with a `/invite` token handoff, and paid intent is intercepted just-in-time by the w1/m62 payment dialog. The gaps are all in the **first run after the machinery succeeds**, verified against source:

- **Activation dead-end.** Registration lands on `/`, which for a new workspace renders two lines of text — "No resources yet / Create a service, database, or key value store to get started" — with **no CTA in the empty state itself** (`dashboard/src/routes/index.tsx:252-258`; the page header's generic "New" dropdown at `index.tsx:224-230` is the only affordance, and nothing marks it as the next step). The shared `EmptyState` has no action slot by design (`dashboard/src/common/components/empty-state.tsx:25-46`); Blueprints and Env groups share the shape. No welcome screen, checklist, guided first deploy, or sample CTA exists anywhere — even though the samples-as-first-run-funnel already shipped (w9/m43–m44) and nothing points a new user at it.
- **Silent verification gap.** Kratos issues a session immediately on registration, but invite email-match redemption is refused for unverified addresses with only a server log (`tenancy.go:187-193`). No dashboard surface says "your email is unverified" or offers a resend, and the verification page has no success continuation (`features/auth/pages/verification-page/index.tsx`).
- **Missing `/auth/error` route.** Kratos's `error.ui_url` (`deploy/gitops/base/values/kratos.values.yaml:174-175`) and the Ory client config (`dashboard/src/common/lib/ory/config.ts:41`) both point at `/auth/error`, but the route does not exist — flow errors land on the generic 404 with no recovery.
- **Sign-up drops `next`.** Login honors a guarded `?next=`; registration always navigates to `/` (`features/auth/pages/register-page/index.tsx:47`), losing deep links.
- **`/invite` handoff is invisible and fragile.** A bare spinner; the token lives in per-tab sessionStorage, so a lost round-trip silently drops redemption — failure surfaces only as the absence of a toast.
- **Payment-cancel dead-end.** Cancelling the PAYMENT_REQUIRED dialog rejects the mutation with the paid plan still selected and no hint that the free plan succeeds (`features/usage/context/payment-required.tsx:86-93`).

Exposure today is low (17 first-party workspaces), but this is the same "before open signup" class as the #PSL item: the funnel must work before external users arrive.

### The card requirement (user decision, 2026-08-19)

ADR046 deliberately kept signup card-free and collected the card just-in-time at first paid intent. The decision is now reversed: **the platform is not usable without a bound payment method** — the free tier included. Rationale: every drive-by signup otherwise becomes an unbillable workspace that still accrues metered usage (ADR046 §0's egress/build-seconds problem), free-tier compute abuse costs real money, and card-on-file is the standard abuse gate for compute platforms. The acknowledged cost is signup conversion (ADR046's original reason for card-free — Render's free tier is card-less); accepted deliberately at bex's current stage, where abuse resistance and clean billing matter more than top-of-funnel volume.

One honest bound on the mechanism (2026-08-20 review): the marker (`payment_method_bound_at`, ADR046 §3) is **monotonic**, and card-lifecycle drift is deliberately dunning's job (ADR046 §7) — but a free-tier-only workspace generates no invoices, so dunning never exercises there, and a card bound once then cancelled is never re-checked. The wall is therefore "a card was bound once at first use" (the standard industry gate), not a continuously-verified card-on-file; the abuse rationale above claims no more than that.

**Positioning (user decision, 2026-08-19): hosted bex.co is a paid product; self-hosting is the free path.** Unlike a closed-source SaaS, the card wall is not exclusion but segmentation — a user unwilling to bind a card has a fully legitimate alternative in the open-source project, so the wall prices the _hosted service_, not access to bex-the-software. Two consequences: (1) the wall's intercept UX must offer **"Self-host bex instead" → the GitHub repository** as a first-class exit (D6/D7), not an error dead-end, and the GitHub/self-host path should be a peer entry point wherever signup is offered; (2) the `all` mode is enabled **before open signup** — with zero external users today, now is the cheapest moment to build the wall (no grandfathering, no mid-flight policy change), and open signup should not launch without it.

**Evaluation without commitment → a future demo mode (user direction, 2026-08-19).** The one audience the two walls genuinely underserve is the evaluator who only wants to _look at the dashboard_ before committing an email verification or a card. The designated answer is a **dedicated demo mode** — a sandboxed, read-only/simulated product tour environment — not any loosening of either gate: the walls stay absolute, and "just looking" gets its own deliberately-built path. Demo mode is **out of scope for this ADR**; it is recorded here so the D7/D8 rationale ("no legitimate business inside") is read as "no legitimate business in the _real_ product — the look-and-see need is served separately," and it becomes its own planned milestone when scheduled.

### Enforcing email verification like Render (research, 2026-08-19)

**Render's shape.** Render requires email verification for full platform access: the dashboard serves a dedicated wall at `dashboard.render.com/verify-email`, and independent onboarding tutorials (2022–2026) consistently describe sign-up → "verify your email" as a required early step — including for GitHub/Google OAuth signups, which must still supply and verify an email ("You need to verify your email to get full access to the platform"). Render's public docs do not publish the exact restriction matrix, but the observable semantics are: **you can sign up and log in, and usage is gated until verified** — a wall inside the product, not a login refusal.

**bex's available mechanisms** (verified against source and Ory docs):

- **Option A — Kratos-native `require_verified_address` login hook** (`selfservice.flows.login.after.{password,oidc}.hooks`, [Ory hook catalog](https://www.ory.com/docs/kratos/hooks/configure-hooks#available-actions)): refuses to issue a session at login until the address is verified, routing the user into the verification flow. Three deltas from the Render shape: (1) registration's `session` hook (`deploy/gitops/base/values/kratos.values.yaml:147-150`) still signs a fresh signup in immediately — the hook only bites at the _next_ login, so adopting it also means removing the registration session hook (signup drops into the verification flow; the first session is minted only by a post-verification login); (2) it is a **login refusal**, not Render's logged-in wall — an unverified user gets no session at all, so they cannot reach authenticated surfaces (billing for D7's card step, invite redemption, settings) and every surface is blocked rather than just usage; (3) it says nothing about already-issued sessions (bounded: revoke unverified identities' sessions at enablement — trivial at 17 first-party workspaces — and Kratos session lifespan caps the residue) or non-login token paths (machine API keys carry no email; third-party human OAuth tokens pass through the Kratos login challenge, so the hook covers them).
- **Option B — bex-api service-layer gate, mirroring D7's PaymentGate.** The auth gate **already resolves verification state on every request**: `core.Identity.EmailVerified` is populated from the Kratos whoami `verifiable_addresses[].verified` match against the trait email (`lego/backend/internal/api/auth.go:558-588`) — the exact signal `BEX_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_INVITE_EMAIL` already consumes for invite redemption (`tenancy.go:187-193`). A gate at the same service-layer seam as D7 refuses usage verbs for unverified human callers with a coded error, reads stay open so the dashboard can render the wall. This is Render's semantics precisely, per-request (covers already-issued sessions), uniform across REST/GraphQL/MCP, and composes with D7 as an ordered pair.

**Option A was adopted at the door on 2026-08-19; revised to a HYBRID on 2026-08-20 (user decision, after walking the implemented flow on dev-6).** The pure at-the-door shape shipped and worked — and the first-run walkthrough exposed its real cost: after sign-up → code entry, the user lands back on a login form and re-enters credentials they created seconds earlier, a forced restart at the single most conversion-critical moment. Re-examined from the user's seat, the "no unverified session" invariant was buying almost nothing there: invite hijack is independently gated by `BEX_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_INVITE_EMAIL`, usage/abuse by D7's card gate, and an unverified session can otherwise only look at an empty dashboard. **The hybrid keeps Option A's mechanism but moves the wall's placement:** the registration `session` hook is retained (the password the user just typed IS the authentication), `show_verification_ui` routes the fresh signup into the verification flow **while signed in** (verify in place, continue seamlessly — no re-login), and `require_verified_address` stays on both login methods as the **backstop**: a stale unverified account cannot start a new session, so an unverified identity cannot persist beyond its registration session. Option B's service-layer usage gate (`EMAIL_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`) remains **deferred, not built**: D7's card gate is the operative usage gate, and the deferral is recorded as the follow-up if verification must ever become a hard per-request usage precondition.

## Decision

Dashboard-first; the backend change is D7's gate-mode extension. D8 is Kratos GitOps config + auth-page flow handling, not a backend gate. No auth-architecture change.

- **D1 — Actionable empty states.** Give `EmptyState` an action slot and wire the primary CTA into the new-user-path empties (Overview, Blueprints, Env groups) instead of dead text.
- **D2 — Getting-started checklist on Overview.** A dismissible card for workspaces with zero resources, led by **"Verify your email"** while the caller's address is unverified (revised 2026-08-20: under the D8 hybrid a signed-in-but-unverified state exists — briefly in the normal flow, longer if the user navigates away from the verification page — and the checklist is its in-app nudge, replacing the deleted banner), then **"Add a payment method"** (D7), then: create first service, deploy a sample (reuses the w9/m43–m44 funnel), connect GitHub, invite a teammate. Dismissal persists client-side; no backend state, no tour library.
- **D3 — Continuity through the auth boundary.** Sign-up honors the same guarded `next` as login, threaded **client-side across the D8 restructure**: the sign-up and login pages stash the safeNext-normalized target in a same-tab sessionStorage relay (the hop into `/auth/verification` is an Elements-internal redirect whose URL Kratos builds, so a query param cannot survive it; the relay re-validates at read so a tampered stash can never redirect), and verification success navigates to that target (revised 2026-08-20: **into the product directly** — the registration session is already held; a session-less visitor is bounced by `requireAuth` to `/auth/login` with the same `next` preserved). Continuity is perceived, not just mechanical: the happy path contains no re-login and no page whose copy denies the journey. The "unverified email" in-app nudge lives in D2's checklist (the standalone banner stays deleted). The invite email-match gate (`BEX_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_INVITE_EMAIL`) stays on — load-bearing under the hybrid (see D8).
- **D4 — Build the missing `/auth/error` route.** Render the Kratos error with a restart-flow link; the configured error UI must never 404.
- **D5 — Visible `/invite` handoff.** Explicit accepting/accepted/failed states and a lost-token recovery path.
- **D6 — Payment-cancel explains the requirement and offers the self-host exit.** Cancelling the PAYMENT_REQUIRED dialog states plainly that a card is required for any resource (there is no free-tier bypass to hint at, D7) **and offers "Self-host bex instead" → the GitHub repository** as the legitimate no-card path (Context § Positioning) — a diversion exit, not a bare rejection.
- **D7 — Card binding is a mandatory onboarding step, gated for all usage.** The ADR046 `PaymentGate` gains an `all` mode (`BEX_REQUIRE_PAYMENT_METHOD=all`; `1` keeps today's paid-intent-only behavior): every Service/Postgres/KeyValue create or Blueprint apply — **any plan, free included** — requires the local `payment_method_bound_at` marker, refused with the same 402/`PAYMENT_REQUIRED` across REST/GraphQL/MCP. The existing just-in-time interception dialog (w1/m62) is the UX that resolves it, so the wall is still "interception, not a dead end": a cardless user's first create attempt opens Checkout inline and resumes — and the same dialog carries the D6 **"Self-host bex instead" → GitHub** exit for users who will not bind a card. Dashboard empty-state CTAs (D1) and the checklist (D2) route through billing while the workspace is unbound. **Production sets `all`, enabled before open signup** (Context § Positioning: build the wall while there are no external users to grandfather). Two scope corrections from the 2026-08-20 review: (1) **agent sessions** — create/steer admission today runs only the dunning gate (`RequireBillingMutation`, `lego/backend/internal/agentsessions/service.go`), while the card check fires later inside background dispatch (`createResolved`'s `RequirePaymentMethod`), so a cardless session is admitted and then dies async as "sandbox create failed" with no 402 for the dialog to intercept; D7 adds the marker check at agent-session admission so the refusal is synchronous and interceptable, keeping the "interception, not a dead end" promise on the ADR048 centerpiece surface. (2) **Enablement ops** — flipping `all` turns every existing unbound workspace refused-on-next-create; the enablement step comps the first-party workspaces (Mode-B — the gate's `AllowsPaidIntent` already honors the exemption) or binds their cards, recorded as part of the rollout rather than left to surprise. The new `all` value is mirrored in `.env.example` and the CLAUDE.md env table per the repo rule.
- **D8 — Email verification as a seamless in-product step, with a login-refusal backstop (Kratos-native; revised 2026-08-20).** Registration keeps its `session` hooks — the just-typed password is the authentication — and adds **`show_verification_ui`** on `registration.after.<method>.hooks` (implementation finding, w6/m42: this hook is what emits the verification `continue_with`; with the after-hooks merely emptied, Kratos falls back to a bare redirect to the default return URL and the user never sees the verification page). A fresh signup therefore lands on `/auth/verification` **holding its session**, enters the emailed code, and continues straight into the product (the guarded `next` deep link or `/`) — no re-login anywhere in the happy path. `require_verified_address` is enabled on **both** login method hooks — `selfservice.flows.login.after.password.hooks` in `deploy/gitops/base/values/kratos.values.yaml` **and** `login.after.oidc` in the out-of-band `oidc.yaml` fragment written by `scripts/auth-secrets.sh` (the hooks exist in BOTH places or the config drifts) — as the **backstop**: a stale unverified account cannot start a new session, so an unverified identity cannot outlive its registration session. The OIDC path is hooked symmetrically, **not trusted**: the Jsonnet claims→traits mapper maps only `email`/`name` and marks nothing verified; whether a GitHub address arrives verified is Kratos's provider claims handling (`email_verified`), and either way the outcome is safe — a provider-verified address passes both hooks untouched (`show_verification_ui` emits nothing when no verification flow was created), an unverified one completes the same code flow. Dashboard flow handling: the register page stashes the guarded `next` and lets Elements follow `continue_with` to `/auth/verification`; verification success navigates into the product, and a session-less visitor on that same navigation is bounced by `requireAuth` to `/auth/login` with `next` preserved (the backstop path's UX). Edges: (1) **recovery** mints a session outside login's after-hooks — verified on Kratos v26.2.0 (w6/m42 t006): completing recovery marks the recovering address verified, so the backstop invariant ("no unverified identity persists across sessions") holds; re-probe on every Kratos bump. (2) The **courier remains on the activation critical path** (the code email gates progression, though no longer the session): the courier-health alert and the visible resend affordance on `/auth/verification` stay required. The invite email-match gate (`BEX_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_INVITE_EMAIL`) stays on — under the hybrid an unverified session CAN exist briefly, so that gate is load-bearing, not just defense in depth. No bex-api gate, no `BEX_REQUIRE_VERIFIED_EMAIL` env, no `EMAIL_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` error code — enforcement is Kratos config (git-tracked values **plus** the script-written Secret fragment); Option B's service-layer usage gate stays a recorded deferral. Enable before open signup, alongside D7's `all` mode.

## Non-goals

- No server-side registration hook (ADR046 records it as rejected; D8 uses only Kratos-native config — the `session` + `show_verification_ui` registration hooks and the `require_verified_address` login backstop — and no custom Kratos code).
- No OAuth-izing the first-party dashboard (`.pm/DO_NOT_DO.md` hard anti-goal); Kratos sessions stay.
- No product-tour/wizard framework, no per-user onboarding state in the control-plane DB (D2 is deliberately client-side). **Demo mode** — the designated look-and-see path for no-commitment evaluators (Context § Positioning) — is future work and explicitly not built here; neither gate is loosened in anticipation of it.
- No change to invite security semantics (verified-email invite gate stays on — load-bearing under the D8 hybrid; ADR024) or to the ADR046 billing mechanism (Checkout/Portal/marker/emitter — only the gate's scope widens, D7).

## Consequences

- A new account's first session offers a working path to a first deploy without leaving the dashboard; the signup→activate path has no dead-end screens.
- **Activation is one continuous flow with two mandatory gates inside it:** sign-up (session minted) → verify email in place (D8 — seamless code entry, no re-login; the login-refusal backstop means an unverified identity cannot outlive its registration session) → bind card (D7 — the w1/m62 interception dialog resolves it inline at first create, and its cancel path diverts no-card users to self-hosting on GitHub as a first-class exit) → first deploy.
- **Open-signup gate:** the D7 `all` mode and D8 gate are launch preconditions for open signup — enabling them afterwards would be a mid-flight policy change on real users.
- **Funnel observability is part of the launch precondition** (2026-08-20 review): activation is now a three-step funnel (verify → bind → deploy) with no measurement defined; before open signup, minimal counters must exist — identities unverified >24h, workspaces unbound >24h, workspaces at zero resources — no new framework, just numbers readable at launch time.
- Implementation is split by risk profile (2026-08-20, superseding the original two-milestone split): **the two launch gates (D7 `all` mode + D8 verification-at-the-door) are their own w6 milestone, materialized 2026-08-20 as `w6/m42`** — a policy flip with an ops step and a rollback story must not ride a dashboard-polish review; the funnel counters are `w6/024` — while **first-run onboarding & actionable empty states** (D1–D3 + the card-led checklist) and **auth & onboarding edge dead-ends** (D4–D6) remain pending `/pm` materialization. Only D7 touches REST/GraphQL/MCP error surfaces (widened 402 scope); D8 is Kratos config + dashboard auth pages, so the parity closing task is carried by D7 alone.
- Two stale ADR018 ledger rows in this area (verification page "unbuilt" — shipped 2026-07-12; workspace-lifecycle "no payment step" — predates w1/m62) are swept by the open w2/m79 re-baseline, not by this ADR.
